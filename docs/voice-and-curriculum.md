# Voice & curriculum — Days 11-30

Read this before writing anything. It is the contract for the second half of the course.

## 1. Who is speaking

**Thach Nguyen Ngoc**, born 2000, Vietnamese backend engineer, ~4 years experience.
Working in English daily (Japanese enterprise environment), currently job hunting.

Days 1-10 were workplace English. **Days 11-30 are everyday conversational English** —
the language you need to actually *live* in an English-speaking place, not just work in.

Keep the same person as Days 1-10. The stories are still his, but now they are about:

- what he does on a Saturday, not what happened in the stand-up
- what he thinks about things, not what he shipped

## 2. The hard rule: vocabulary you already know

This is the single most important constraint in the whole course.

**The dialogue lines must only use words he already has.** He is A1-A2 grammar,
B1 technical vocabulary. He does NOT have everyday B1-B2 conversational vocabulary.

This sounds like a limitation. It is the entire design. He is *activating* words,
not learning them. A new word may appear **only** in a 💡 box.

## 4. The long story is the anchor — write it FIRST

It sets the vocabulary and the mood for the other five conversations.
450-550 words, one continuous narrative, past tense, with dialogue in quotes
inside the prose so it sounds like speech.

Shape that works every time:

**morning → small problem → what I did → what happened → what I felt → what I learned**

Keep it honest. Boring is good. *"I went to the market and bought rice"* is a
perfect story if you say it well.

## 6. The 20-day arc

The point is **range**: a single topic teaches you one conversation. Different
topics force different sentence shapes, and that is what makes you sound like a
person instead of a book.

**Block 1 — the basics you need every week (11-17)**
- 11 Coffee Shop — ordering, saying what you want, changing your order
- 12 Grocery Shopping — asking for things, comparing, choosing
- 13 Taking a Bus — directions, stops, times, understanding announcements
- 14 Phone and Messages — calls, texts, voice messages, missed calls
- 15 Weekend Plans — invitations, making plans, saying no politely
- 16 Weather and Seasons — describing weather, small talk, complaining lightly
- 17 Cooking at Home — ingredients, steps, tastes, failing at something

**Block 2 — adult life (18-24)**
- 18 Money and Prices — paying, counting change, splitting the bill, asking price
- 19 Health and Body — symptoms, seeing a doctor, advice, recovering
- 20 Housing and Rent — looking at a place, problems, neighbours, cleaning
- 21 Travel and Places — stations, tickets, directions abroad, small problems
- 22 Likes and Dislikes — opinions, preferences, softeners, changing your mind
- 23 Agreeing and Disagreeing — saying no kindly, partly agreeing, moving on
- 24 Asking for Help — asking a stranger, a shop, a friend, politely and firmly

**Block 3 — the hard, human stuff (25-30)**
- 25 Losing Your Way — being lost, admitting it, getting out of it
- 26 Warnings and Caution — telling someone no, giving advice, bad news
- 27 Workplace Small Talk — the office, day 30, still light, NOT deep work
- 28 Hobbies and Interests — what you do for fun, learning something new
- 29 Storytelling — telling about your life, past experiences, "one time..."
- 30 Sounding Natural — fillers, accents, rhythm, the last exam of the course

Day 27 is deliberately workplace. It is the one bridge back to Days 1-10, and the
lesson sits between the light days and the deep ones.

## 7. English quality bar

- **A1-A2 grammar throughout.** Short sentences. Do not write B1-B2 sentences.
- **Do not write childish English.** He is an adult engineer. Keep the *language*
  simple and the *content* adult.
- **Sentences vary.** Mix 3-word and 12-word sentences. All-medium is a robot.
- **Contractions everywhere:** *I'm, that's, didn't, I'd, it's, we're, don't,
  can't, won't*. Everyday speech is full of them.
- **One idea per sentence.** Do not join two ideas with *because* or *and* in a
  way that needs a big clause.
- Real, specific details beat generic ones. *"Eleven dollars for a coffee"*,
  not *"It was expensive."*

## 8. Voice — the thing that makes him sound natural

These are the real lessons of Days 11-30. Use them every day.

**Fillers** (sounds human, costs nothing):
`I mean... · well... · actually... · you know... · kind of... · sort of... ·
like... · anyway...`

**Softening** (a soft opinion in English is not weak):
`I think... · I guess... · probably... · maybe... · a little bit`

**Getting attention / agreeing to keep talking:**
`Oh really? · Really? · That sounds interesting. · What happened? ·
Wait, really? · Tell me more.`

**Wrapping up:**
`That's all. · Anyway... · That's it. · I should go. · See you tomorrow.`
