# Day 25 · Conversation 5 — Getting There Late Because You Were Lost

## 🎬 Situation
You're late and it's your fault. English lets you explain without writing a paragraph of apology, and there's a one-sentence version that works every time.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** You're late!

> **You:** I know. I'm sorry — I got completely turned around.

> **Friend:** Are you all right?

> **You:** I'm fine. I just went the wrong way twice, which is a new low.

> **Friend:** How bad are we talking?

> **You:** About twenty minutes. I could suggest a coffee while we wait.

> **Friend:** I'll have one. You've earned it.

> **You:** Fair.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I got completely turned around** — **"turned around"** not *"lost"*. It's a nicer way to say it, and slightly funny
- **which is a new low** — self-deprecating humour about your own mistake. Extremely common and it defuses the awkwardness
- **how bad are we talking?** — asking how late you are, in words rather than minutes. Very natural
- **which is a new low** — again, the key move: making fun of yourself before anyone else can

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I know. I'm sorry — I got completely turned around. — How bad are we talking? — [number]. I could suggest ___.*

⚠️ **"How bad are we talking?"** is a phrase worth having. It asks for a number while acknowledging the other person is annoyed. Compare: *"How late am I?"* (direct, slightly defensive) — the informal version invites them to be honest with you instead of performing politeness.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can apologise once and then be funny about it
- …you can ask how late you are without sounding defensive
- …you can say *"which is a new low"* 🎯
