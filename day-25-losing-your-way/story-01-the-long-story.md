# Day 25 · 📖 The Long Story — The Day I Walked For Four Hours

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I walked for four hours once because I would not ask for directions in
English.** That is the short version. The slightly more embarrassing version is
that I had a phone in my pocket with a working map, and I did not open it,
because opening it would have meant admitting that I did not know where I was.

It was a Tuesday. I had been at a workshop that finished forty minutes earlier
than I'd expected, and I decided to walk, because walking was free and I wanted
to look at the town. That's all the planning there was. I did not check the map
before I left, and I did not check it after about an hour, and by then I was in
a part of the town I could not have drawn.

**The reason I didn't ask anyone is the reason that matters.** I'm quite sure I
could have asked. I've thought about it a lot. I could have said "excuse me, do
you know where the station is," and every single person on that street would have
answered, and half of them would have walked me there. What stopped me was not a
language gap. It was that I had decided — somewhere early and without noticing —
that being the sort of person who asks is a different sort of person from the one
I'd decided I was going to be that afternoon.

So I walked. And it was genuinely pleasant for the first hour. I got turned around twice, both times because I ignored a street sign that said exactly what I needed. By the third hour I was just committed to it, which is its own kind of stupid, and by the fourth I was reading shop names to work out where I was.

I eventually got back by following a river, which I recognised, and then a bus that went vaguely in the right direction. I was home at nine, having left at four, and I was not happy about it.

What I learned is that the cost was never the asking. Every hour of that walk was cheaper than the ten seconds of saying *"I'm lost — which way is the station?"* and every one of those hours happened because I wanted to protect a version of myself that nobody was watching and nobody cared about.

I ask now. It's still slightly uncomfortable, which tells me nothing useful, and it takes four seconds.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I walked for four hours once because I would not ___. That's the short version. The more embarrassing version is that I had ___ and I did not ___, because ___. — What I learned is that ___.*

Present perfect in the opening (*"would not ask"*) sets up a pattern you still live with. The repeated past (*"I had a phone… and I did not open it"*) is what makes the second version feel like the real confession.
