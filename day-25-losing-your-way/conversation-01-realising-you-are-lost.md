# Day 25 · Conversation 1 — Realising You Are Lost

## 🎬 Situation
You don't know where you are. English gives you a way to say it that doesn't involve dramatic hand-waving or pretending you knew.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Sorry — I think I'm lost. This isn't the road I wanted, is it?

> **Other:** Where were you trying to get to?

> **You:** The station. But I've been walking for about twenty minutes and nothing looks right.

> **Other:** Okay, so you're on Church Street. The station's about ten minutes that way.

> **You:** This way? Are you sure?

> **Other:** Yeah — go to the end, turn right at the traffic lights, and you'll see it.

> **You:** Okay. Thank you very much, genuinely.

> **Other:** No problem. It's an easy mistake to make.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I think I'm lost** — the honest opener. **"I think"** rather than *"I'm completely lost"*, which sounds more dramatic than you are
- **this isn't the road I wanted, is it?** — making the shared assumption explicit, which gets you real help
- **nothing looks right** — **"looks right"** is what you say when a place isn't familiar. Very natural
- **that way?** — repeating the direction to confirm it. **"This way?"** sounds doubtful; **"that way?"** sounds checking

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Sorry — I think I'm lost. This isn't the road I wanted, is it? — I'm trying to get to ___, but nothing looks right.*

⚠️ **"Where were you trying to get to?"** is the question every local asks first. Answer it before they have to ask twice, and you'll get directions instead of sympathy. The order matters: *admit lost → name destination → describe the problem.*

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can say you're lost without embarrassment
- …you can give a stranger enough information to help you
- …you can confirm a direction with *"that way?"* 🎯
