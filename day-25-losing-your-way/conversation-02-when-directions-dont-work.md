# Day 25 · Conversation 2 — When The Directions Don't Work

## 🎬 Situation
You've been given clear directions and you're still lost. This happens constantly, and English has a precise way to say *"that clearly isn't where you meant."*

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Right, so I'm at the end of Church Street, at the traffic lights.

> **Other:** And you turned right?

> **You:** I did. And then there's a car park, not a station.

> **Other:** Ah — that's probably the one on Marlborough Road.

> **You:** So I've come too far?

> **Other:** Not far. About five minutes back.

> **You:** Okay. So I walk back to the lights and then left instead?

> **Other:** Yeah — sorry, I said right, I meant left.

> **You:** No problem at all. Thanks for checking.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **right, so I'm at…** — **"right, so"** to confirm what you have before you describe what's wrong. Extremely useful when instructions fail
- **that clearly isn't where you meant** — the most useful sentence in this conversation. **"Where you meant"** puts the error with the speaker, politely
- **so I've come too far?** — checking the scale of the mistake. Locals always answer this, and it's reassuring
- **I said right, I meant left** — the honest correction. Notice the speaker owns it, which is why *"no problem at all"* is easy to say

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Right, so I'm at ___. — I did turn right, but there's ___, not ___. — That clearly isn't where you meant. — So I've come too far?*

⚠️ **"That clearly isn't where you meant"** is the sentence to memorise. Beginners say *"You gave me wrong directions"*, which is a complaint. This version reports what happened **and** leaves the other person room to correct themselves, which they immediately do.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can report that instructions didn't work without blaming anyone
- …you can use *"that clearly isn't where you meant"*
- …you can ask how far wrong you are 🎯
