# Day 25 · Conversation 3 — Using Your Phone As The Backup

## 🎬 Situation
You've got a map, a street name you can't read, and ten minutes. English lets you do this without switching languages, which matters more than it sounds.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me — do you know if this is Marlborough Road?

> **Other:** Yeah, that runs parallel. You want that one?

> **You:** I think so. The map says the station's on it, but I can't tell if this is it.

> **Other:** Are you at the junction with the petrol station?

> **You:** Yeah — the one with the big sign.

> **Other:** Then this isn't it. That's two streets over.

> **You:** Right. So if I go back to the lights and turn left, then right at the post office?

> **Other:** That's the one. You've got about five minutes.

> **You:** Brilliant. Thank you.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **does this run parallel?** — describing a road alongside another. **"Runs parallel"** is exactly the right phrase
- **you want that one?** — checking you have the right street. Very useful when names are similar
- **the map says it's on it, but…** — **"but"** doing the honest work: you're not sure, and saying so gets better help
- **then right at the post office** — **"then"** is the word that holds a multi-step instruction together

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Do you know if this is ___ Road? — The map says ___, but I can't tell if it's this one. — So if I ___, then ___?*

⚠️ **Say "but I can't tell."** It sounds like a weakness and it does the opposite: it tells the other person that your confirmation is unreliable, so they check properly instead of guessing. A confident wrong answer gets you lost for another twenty minutes.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can describe what you see to confirm a location
- …you can admit uncertainty with *"but I can't tell"*
- …you can repeat multi-step instructions back to check them 🎯
