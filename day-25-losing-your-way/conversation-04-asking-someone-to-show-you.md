# Day 25 · Conversation 4 — Asking Someone To Show You

## 🎬 Situation
Directions have failed twice. Asking someone to walk you there is a bigger ask than it sounds — and English has an exact script for it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me — sorry to ask, but would you have two minutes to show me where the station is?

> **Other:** Yeah, sure. I was going that way anyway.

> **You:** That's very kind, thank you.

> **Other:** No problem. It's about five minutes.

> **You:** I'm meeting someone at six, so I'm a bit — yeah.

> **Other:** Don't worry, we're nearly there.

> **You:** You don't have to wait, if you'd rather not.

> **Other:** It's fine. Honestly, it's on my way.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **would you have two minutes to show me…?** — **"two minutes"** makes the ask small and specific. Much easier to say than *"could you help me?"*
- **I'd rather not** — giving the other person an easy out, so they can decline without it being awkward. **"I'd rather not"** not *"I don't want to"*
- **that's on my way** — the honest reason to help. Locals say it because it lets both of you pretend it was convenient
- **I'm meeting someone at six, so I'm a bit…** — starting to trail off. You don't have to finish the sentence

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Would you have two minutes to show me ___? — That's very kind. — You don't have to wait, if you'd rather not.*

⚠️ **Always offer the other person a way out.** *"You don't have to wait"* costs you nothing and means they can say no safely — which paradoxically makes it more likely they say yes. A request they can decline is not a trap.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask someone to walk with you using a specific time
- …you can offer them an exit without sounding like you expect refusal
- …you can trail off naturally: *"I'm a bit…"* 🎯
