# Day 24 · Conversation 1 — Asking A Stranger, Small Thing

## 🎬 Situation
You need something that isn't an emergency. This is the hardest category, because English politeness makes small requests sound almost apologetic — and there's a point where that goes too far.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me — do you know if this train stops at Reading?

> **Other:** Yeah, it does. Third one along the platform.

> **You:** Thank you. Sorry, one more thing — do you have the time?

> **Other:** It's about half two.

> **You:** That's fine, I've got a bit of slack.

> **Other:** No worries.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **do you know if ___?** — the softest way to ask for information. **"Do you know"** rather than *"can you tell me"*, which is a request, not a question
- **excuse me — do you know…** — the standard stranger opener. **"Sorry to bother you"** is a little too much for this level
- **that's fine, I've got a bit of slack** — **"slack"** means room to spare. Very useful and work-appropriate
- **that's fine** — answering your own concern before the other person has to. Saves them the effort

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Excuse me — do you know if ___? — Thank you. Sorry, one more thing — do you have the time?*

⚠️ **How much apology is right?** For one small question: **"excuse me"**, once. For a second question to the same person: **"sorry, one more thing"**. For a real interruption: **"sorry to bother you"**. Beyond that — *"sorry, I'm so sorry, I know you're busy but..."* — you're apologising for existing, and English speakers find that uncomfortable rather than polite.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask a stranger a question without over-apologising
- …you can chain a second question with *"one more thing"*
- …you can say *"I've got a bit of slack"* 🎯
