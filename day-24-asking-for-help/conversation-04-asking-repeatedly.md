# Day 24 · Conversation 4 — Asking For Something You Need Repeatedly

## 🎬 Situation
You need the same favour often enough that it becomes part of the relationship. English has a specific way to do this — and it's about the mechanism, not the charm.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** You don't have to ask, honestly. Just text me.

> **You:** I know. I will — I just wanted to ask properly the first time.

> **Friend:** You've asked me twice.

> **You:** I know. That's why I stopped asking for the big things.

> **Friend:** What do you mean?

> **You:** The big things I ask. The small things I tell you. It stops you having to think about me.

> **Friend:** That's actually smart. Most people do it the other way round.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **you don't have to ask** — someone telling you to stop asking. Common with close friends
- **I wanted to ask properly the first time** — **"properly"** means formally, respectfully. Exactly the right word
- **that's why I stopped asking for the big things** — an unexpectedly good negotiation, and true
- **the small things I tell you** — **"the small things I tell you"** vs *"ask you"*. The distinction is the whole point

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *You don't have to ask. — I wanted to ask properly the first time. — That's why I stopped asking for the big things. — The big things I ask; the small things I tell you.*

⚠️ **The useful pattern: ask for the big things, inform about the small things.** Say *"I'm going to be out late tonight"* rather than *"is it okay if I go out?"* The first assumes consent; the second asks for permission every single time, which quietly implies you don't trust the relationship.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask for a favour without apologising for asking
- …you can switch from asking permission to giving notice for small things
- …you can say *"I wanted to ask properly the first time"* 🎯
