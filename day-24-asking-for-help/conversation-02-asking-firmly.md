# Day 24 · Conversation 2 — Asking For Help, Firmly

## 🎬 Situation
You need real help. Being too polite makes you unclear, and being too direct sounds rude. The answer is precise language, not volume.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi — I hope you can help. I've locked my bike to this rail and the key's snapped.

> **Other:** Ah. That's annoying.

> **You:** Is there anywhere near here that could cut it?

> **Other:** There's a locksmith on the high street, about five minutes that way.

> **You:** Do you know what they charge? I don't want to be surprised.

> **Other:** I think it's around forty, but check first.

> **You:** Okay. Thank you — that was really helpful.

> **Other:** No problem. Hope you get it sorted.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I hope you can help** — a good opener when the request is real. It's warm and it signals *this is a real problem*
- **the key's snapped** — **"snapped"** is the natural verb for a key breaking. **"broken"** is correct but vague
- **could cut it?** — asking whether a tool *could* do something, not whether it will. **"Could…"** leaves room for a yes
- **I don't want to be surprised** — asking about cost **before** agreeing. Extremely sensible and extremely common

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I hope you can help. — Is there anywhere near here that could ___? — Do you know what they charge? I don't want to be surprised.*

⚠️ **"I don't want to be surprised"** is the sentence to learn. It asks for a price without sounding miserly, because the reason you give is *avoiding a shock*, not *avoiding expense*. English speakers ask for prices constantly — **it's considered sensible, not rude.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask for real help without over-apologising
- …you can ask about cost before committing
- …you can use *"I hope you can help"* for a genuine problem 🎯
