# Day 24 · Conversation 3 — Explaining The Problem To Someone Paid To Help

## 🎬 Situation
A barber, a receptionist, a repair shop. These people are busy. The most useful thing you can do is describe the problem clearly and stop talking.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Staff:** What can I do for you?

> **You:** My boiler's leaking. There's water coming out underneath it.

> **Staff:** How long has it been doing that?

> **You:** Since Sunday morning. I put a towel down but it's still going.

> **Staff:** And you've turned the water off at the mains?

> **You:** Yes, last night.

> **Staff:** Right. That'll be a call-out plus parts. Can I have the postcode?

> **You:** Yeah, it's OX1.

> **Staff:** Okay. So what we do is we'll turn the water back on and I'll send someone tomorrow morning.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **what can I do for you?** — how tradespeople and staff open. Then **stop talking** and let them ask
- **the boiler's leaking** — **"leaking"** is the natural verb; *"there's a leak from the boiler"* sounds like a report
- **it's still going** — **"still going"** for something continuing. Very common and very easy
- **a call-out plus parts** — **"a call-out"** is the visit fee. A word you will meet, and one that belongs to this world only

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *[Thing]'s leaking. — There's water coming out of ___. — Since ___. — [what you already did]. — Can I have the ___?*

⚠️ **Answer, then stop.** Workers in English are trained to ask one question at a time; giving them five pieces of information unprompted makes their job harder, not easier. Describe the problem, say what you've tried, and let them ask.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can describe a fault in two sentences
- …you can say what you already tried
- …you can answer short questions without over-explaining 🎯
