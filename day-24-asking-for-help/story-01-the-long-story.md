# Day 24 · 📖 The Long Story — The Thing I Couldn't Ask For

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**There is one person in this country whose help I have needed about ten times in
two years, and I asked properly every single time.** I want to say that as a
compliment to them, because it is one. It is also an indictment of me, and I have
only worked out why in the last few months.

For about a year I could not ask anyone for anything that took more than two
minutes. If I needed a document signed, I would work around it for four days
first and then find a way to do it myself that was worse. If I had a question
about the code, I would spend an hour looking at it alone first, so that when I
finally asked it wouldn't sound as if I couldn't find the answer myself. Every one
of those delays cost me half a day, and I spent them all for the same reason.

**The reason was that asking felt like admitting something.** In my first
language, asking someone for help is completely normal and carries no charge at
all. Nobody thinks less of you for it. Translating that into English, I assumed
the charge had come with the language, and so I built a system around not
needing anything — which, as systems go, was both expensive and completely
unnecessary.

Then a colleague asked me a favour in about the same week I asked her one. Her
version was one message: *"can you look at this for me?"* No context, no
apology, no reason she couldn't have done it herself. And I said yes
instantly, because it cost her nothing to ask and everything to me.

So now I ask. Not confidently — I'm still not good at it — but I ask in a
sentence, in the first hour, instead of working around it for four days. That's
the entire change and it has been worth more than any amount of English study.

What I learned is that the awkwardness I was avoiding was almost entirely mine.
Nobody in this country has ever thought less of me for asking. What I had
actually been avoiding was not their opinion. It was the feeling of asking.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *There is one person ___ whose help I have needed ___. For about a year I could not ___. The reason was that ___. — Then ___ asked me a favour in the same week. So now I ___. What I learned is that ___.*

Past perfect for the state before the change (*"I had needed"* / *"I was avoiding"*), simple past for the sequence. And note the ending names the feeling as **yours** — that's what makes it an insight rather than an excuse.
