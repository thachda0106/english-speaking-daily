# Day 24 · Conversation 5 — When The Help Doesn't Come

## 🎬 Situation
You asked, and nothing happened. English lets you follow up without turning it into a complaint — and there's a big difference between chasing and nagging.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi — just following up on this. No rush at all.

> **Them:** Oh, sorry. It's been a crazy week. I forgot.

> **You:** No problem. Do you have a sense of when you can?

> **Them:** Thursday, I think. If that still works for you.

> **You:** Thursday's fine. And if it slips again, just tell me — no need to apologise.

> **Them:** Appreciated. It won't.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **just following up on this** — the standard chase-up. **"Following up"** is a noun and a verb in English, and it's completely neutral
- **no rush at all** — removing pressure from the message. Slightly disarming, and it works
- **do you have a sense of when you can?** — asking for a rough date. **"a sense of"** is the natural hedge
- **if it slips again, just tell me** — taking the blame for future lateness away. Very generous and very efficient

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Just following up on this. — No rush. — Do you have a sense of when you can? — If it slips again, just tell me.*

⚠️ **The difference between chasing and nagging, in one sentence:**

| Nagging | Chasing |
|---|---|
| *Any update?* | *Just following up — do you have a sense of when?* |
| *You said last week* | *No rush. Do you have a rough date?* |
| *Still nothing* | *If it slips, just tell me — no need to apologise* |

**Chasing asks for a date. Nagging asks why they haven't answered.** Only one of those gets you what you want.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can follow up without sounding passive-aggressive
- …you can ask for a rough date
- …you can give the other person a way to be late without apologising 🎯
