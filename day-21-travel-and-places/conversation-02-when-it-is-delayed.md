# Day 21 · Conversation 2 — When It Is Delayed

## 🎬 Situation
Your train isn't running. Announcements go past too fast to catch, and this is when a small amount of vocabulary saves an hour.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me, what's the delay?

> **Staff:** There's a signal problem outside Reading. We're maybe twenty minutes.

> **You:** Twenty minutes — do we know if that's final?

> **Staff:** Not yet. They update every fifteen minutes. There's announcements on platform two.

> **You:** Is there another route if it gets worse?

> **Staff:** Possibly. You'd change at Swindon, but it's two hours longer.

> **You:** I'll wait for now, then. Thanks for explaining.

> **Staff:** I'll let the driver know — people sometimes get left on the wrong platform.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **a signal problem** — the standard reason for a delay. **"signals"** is usually the answer, whatever the real cause
- **do we know if that's final?** — **"final"** is the key word. **"Is that the final time?"** is the precise question
- **they update every fifteen minutes** — **"they update"** — the update is about time information. Never *"it updates"*
- **get left on the wrong platform** — **"get left"** = be abandoned. Very common: *I got left there*

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *What's the delay? — Do we know if that's final? — Is there another route if it gets worse?*

⚠️ **"Do we know if that's final?"** is worth memorising as a fixed phrase. Beginners ask *"Is this the last delay?"*, which nobody understands, because the concept is that the number is **not final** — it may change.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask why there's a delay
- …you can ask whether a time estimate is final
- …you can ask about an alternative route and judge the trade-off 🎯
