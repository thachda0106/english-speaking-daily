# Day 21 · Conversation 3 — Asking For Directions

## 🎬 Situation
You need to find somewhere. A good directions request and a good answer have fixed shapes, and knowing both makes you look fluent immediately.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me, is there a pharmacy near here?

> **Other:** Yeah, one minute that way — straight down, then left at the post office.

> **You:** And it's on the left or the right?

> **Other:** Right. Opposite the bank.

> **You:** Thanks very much.

> **Other:** No problem.

> **You:** Sorry, can I just check — I'm looking for the one that's open now. Is that it?

> **Other:** I think so, yeah. There's another further up, but I think that one's shut at six.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **is there a ___ near here?** — the standard opener. **"near here"** rather than *"nearby"*, which is fine in writing
- **one minute that way** — distance in walking time. Far more useful than metres
- **straight down, then left at the ___** — the shape of nearly every direction: **straight → then → landmark**
- **is that it?** — confirming you found it. Short, natural, and exactly what a local expects

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *is there a ___ near here? — [listen] — Thanks very much. — I'm looking for ___, is that it?*

⚠️ **Always check the left/right and the landmark.** *"Straight down"* alone is not enough — the second half of the instruction is where people go wrong. Saying *"and it's on the left?"* out loud is normal and makes you sound attentive, not lost.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask for directions in one sentence
- …you can confirm left/right and a landmark before you leave
- …you can check whether you've found the right place with *"is that it?"* 🎯
