# Day 21 · Conversation 4 — Somewhere New, On Purpose

## 🎬 Situation
You're visiting a place you've never been. English travel conversation has its own genre — the slightly enthusiastic stranger — and it's much easier than you expect.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Other:** First time here?

> **You:** Yeah, first time. A friend said it was worth the trip.

> **Other:** Are you seeing much?

> **You:** Just the old town today. Tomorrow I'm going wherever looks interesting.

> **Other:** Oh, you should go up the hill. The view's the best thing here.

> **You:** Is it a long walk?

> **Other:** About twenty minutes. Steep at the end, but worth it.

> **You:** That's exactly what I like — somewhere that takes effort to see.

> **Other:** Yeah, that's why nobody comes. Best kept secret.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **first time here?** — two words that open most travel conversations with strangers
- **is it a long walk?** — asking about effort, which locals always answer honestly
- **worth it** — the most common judgement in English travel talk. Almost everything is either *"worth it"* or *"not worth it"*
- **somewhere that takes effort to see** — how you describe a place you value. **"takes effort"** implies a reward

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *First time here? — Just the old town today. — Is it a long walk? — [something worth it]. — That's exactly what I like.*

⚠️ **"That's exactly what I like"** is the phrase that makes strangers recommend places to you. Locals volunteer information when they can tell you're interested. This sentence is how you tell them.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can start a conversation with any stranger on public transport
- …you can ask about effort, not just distance
- …you can say *"worth it"* and mean it 🎯
