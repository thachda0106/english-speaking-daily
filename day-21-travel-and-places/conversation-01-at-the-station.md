# Day 21 · Conversation 1 — At The Station

## 🎬 Situation
You're buying a ticket or finding the right platform. English stations have their own vocabulary, and most of it is single words.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me, which platform is it for Oxford?

> **Staff:** Platform three, up the stairs on your left.

> **You:** Is it on the board?

> **Staff:** Yeah, it says Oxford, 11:42.

> **You:** Does it stop there, or is it a change?

> **Staff:** It goes all the way. No need to change.

> **You:** Great. And is there a café on the platform?

> **Staff:** There's one by the stairs, but it shuts at half ten.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **which platform is it for ___?** — **"for"** is required. *"Which platform is Oxford?"* is understood but less natural
- **it's on the board** — **"the board"** is the departures screen. **"the board says…"** is how you check
- **is it a change?** — asking whether you must change trains. **"a change"**, not *"a changing"*; **"change trains"**, never *"change the train"*
- **does it stop there, or is it a change?** — one sentence covering both possibilities

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Which platform is it for ___? — Does it stop there, or is it a change?*

⚠️ **Two words worth getting exactly right:** *change* and *platform* are counted. **"Which change do I want for Oxford?"** is correct — you identify a change by number. And **"platform"**, not *"platforme"*.

- …you can ask for a platform and confirm it against the board
- …you can ask whether a journey needs a change
- …you can ask a practical question while waiting 🎯
