# Day 21 · 📖 The Long Story — The Place I Got On A Train To

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I went somewhere for one day and got on a train for the wrong reason, and it
turned out to be the best thing that happened to me that year.** I have thought
about this a lot, because the version where I planned it would have gone
completely differently.

I was in the city for work, and I had one free day, and every list of things to
do said the same five names. I had seen two of them on previous trips. The third
was a museum I did not want. So I did the thing I have never once regretted
doing and got on a train with no destination, just a name a colleague had
mentioned once — a small town, an hour away, and the promise of a market.

I got it wrong twice before I got anywhere. I got on the train going the wrong
direction, which I only noticed because a man asked whether he could sit there and
I said yes and then thought, no, actually, and had to get off again at the next
stop. That was the first twenty minutes. Then I got on a bus that didn't run on
Sundays, and stood in the rain reading a timetable like someone who had made a
series of decisions on purpose.

**What I remember is the market.** I arrived with no idea what I was doing, and I
spent about four hours there because it rained and I had nowhere to be. I bought
cheese from a man who was extremely patient with me. I had a lunch I'd never
eaten before. And because I had no plan, I stayed for the afternoon and saw a
town I would otherwise have photographed from a train window and forgotten.

The trip back was delayed by about an hour and a half, which would have ruined
it if the day had gone badly. It didn't. I sat in a station café with a book and
felt, for the first time in that city, like I'd actually been somewhere.

I have recommended that town to about fifteen people since. I always say the
same two things: go on a Sunday because it shuts at two, and take the bus rather
than the train, because the bus goes past the good part. Neither of those is the
reason to go.

What I learned is that I only relax when I've given up on the plan. Every day I
have ever enjoyed involved either no plan, or a plan I'd already decided was
failing. The wrong train was not a mistake — it was the first of the good parts.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I went somewhere for one day and ___. What I remember is ___. What I learned is that ___.

Long first sentence with an **"and"** in the middle is the natural shape of an English anecdote — one clause, then the turn. Let the sentence run long; short sentences make it sound like a list.
