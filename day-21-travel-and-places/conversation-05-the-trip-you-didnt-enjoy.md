# Day 21 · Conversation 5 — The Trip You Didn't Enjoy

## 🎬 Situation
You went somewhere and it was disappointing. This is the hardest travel conversation, because you have to criticise something without sounding rude.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** So, how was the trip?

> **You:** Mixed. The town was lovely, but the day out was not what I expected.

> **Friend:** Oh no. What happened?

> **You:** We got there and the main thing — the reason we went — was closed. Permanently.

> **Friend:** Oh no. Did you know before you went?

> **You:** Not that it was closed. I should have checked, but I'd have gone anyway.

> **Friend:** Was the rest of it any good?

> **You:** Actually yes. We ended up in a village outside it that was better.

> **Friend:** Well, that's a win, then.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **mixed** — one word for *"some good, some bad"*. The standard answer to *"how was it?"* when you don't want to lie
- **not what I expected** — the politest way to say it was disappointing. **"Expected"** does the work; you never say *"bad"*
- **I should have checked** — taking responsibility. **"Should have + past participle"** is the English regret formula
- **that was a win** — reframing a disappointment. **"That's a win"** is genuinely common

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Mixed. It was lovely, but ___ was not what I expected. — I should have checked, but I'd have gone anyway. — Actually yes, ___ was better.*

⚠️ **"Mixed" is the answer to learn.** It's honest, short, and instantly understood. Pair it with **"not what I expected"** — never *"it was disappointing"*, which sounds like you're blaming someone.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can answer *"how was it?"* honestly in one word
- …you can criticise without saying *"disappointing"*
- …you can reframe something that went wrong with *"that was a win"* 🎯
