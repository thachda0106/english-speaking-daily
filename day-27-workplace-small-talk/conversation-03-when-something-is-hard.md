# Day 27 · Conversation 3 — When Something Is Hard

## 🎬 Situation
You don't understand something and you have to say so. This is the sentence most people are afraid of, for years. It is also the one that keeps you from wasting a whole day.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Colleague:** Have you started the new report yet?

> **You:** I started it, but one part is hard for me right now.

> **Colleague:** Which part?

> **You:** The part with the old data. I don't know how the table is used, so I'm reading first.

> **Colleague:** That sounds right. Do you want me to explain it?

> **You:** Yes, please, if you have time. Ten minutes would help a lot.

> **Colleague:** Sure. Let's look at it now.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`started · new · report · part · hard · right now · which · old data · table · used · reading · first · sounds · right · want · explain · yes · please · time · ten minutes · help · look`

## ✅ After you speak
- The stuck formula = **what I did + what is hard + the specific thing I need**:
  *"I started it, but one part is hard for me. I don't know how the table is used, so I'm reading first."*
- 💡 **"That sounds right."** — you accept the approach and then ask. Very different from just asking, which can sound like you don't have a plan.
- 💡 **"Yes, please, if you have time."** — 🎯 **the exact phrase that makes asking easy.** *If you have time* is the softener that removes all pressure. Without it, asking feels like a demand. With it, it's a gift you are giving them the right to refuse.
- 💡 **"Ten minutes would help a lot."** — specific beats vague. Ten minutes is easy to say yes to. *"Some help"* is easy to say no to.
- 🎙️ Play `colleague-03` and answer. **Today: ask one real question using "if you have time."**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** an interview is the same conversation with more pressure. *"I don't know X, but here's what I've read so far, and here's what I need"* is the exact answer to *"what would you do if you got stuck?"* — which is asked in nearly every technical interview.
