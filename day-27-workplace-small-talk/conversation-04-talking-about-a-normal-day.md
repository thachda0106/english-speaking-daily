# Day 27 · Conversation 4 — Talking About a Normal Day

## 🎬 Situation
Someone asks what you did today. This is the question with no real content, and the answer that works is a short story with one detail in it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Colleague:** How was your day?

> **You:** Not bad. I finished one small thing, and it was easier than I thought.

> **Colleague:** Nice. What made it easier?

> **You:** I asked Ken about it in the morning, so I didn't get stuck for a long time.

> **Colleague:** That's the trick, isn't it? Asking early saves the afternoon.

> **You:** Exactly. Tomorrow I'll do the same.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`day · not bad · finished · one small thing · easier · thought · made · asked · about · morning · stuck · long time · trick · asking · early · saves · afternoon · exactly · tomorrow · same`

## ✅ After you speak
- The day-answer formula = **short verdict + one thing + why it went well**:
  *"Not bad. I finished one small thing, and it was easier than I thought — because I asked Ken in the morning."*
- 💡 **"Not bad."** — the default good answer. Two words, natural, and much better than *"Fine."* which closes the door.
- 💡 **"That's the trick, isn't it?"** — agreeing out loud with a half-question. Super common in English, and it keeps the conversation alive with almost no vocabulary.
- 💡 **"Asking early saves the afternoon."** — 🎯 a **rhyming** sentence. English small talk loves these. They make you sound relaxed and native-ish without any grammar at all. Learn two or three and reuse them.
- 🎙️ Play `colleague-04` and answer. **Today: answer "How was your day?" in three sentences, out loud, with one detail.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** a good answer to *"How was your day?"* needs **one specific detail** — a name, a number, a time. *"Good, I worked"* is forgettable. *"I finished the report and asked Ken about the old table"* is a story. One detail is the whole trick.
