# Day 27 · Conversation 5 — Leaving Work on Time

## 🎬 Situation
The day ends. Some people leave fast, some stay for hours. This is about closing the day cleanly and not feeling guilty about it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Colleague:** Are you staying late tonight?

> **You:** Maybe an hour. I want to finish this part, then I'll go.

> **Colleague:** You don't have to. It can wait until tomorrow.

> **You:** I know. But I'd rather not leave it half done.

> **Colleague:** That's your call. I'll go first, then.

> **You:** Okay, see you tomorrow. Thanks for today.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`staying · late · tonight · maybe · one hour · want · finish · part · go · don't have to · wait · until tomorrow · know · rather · leave · half done · your call · go first · okay · see you tomorrow · thanks for today`

## ✅ After you speak
- The end-of-day formula = **answer + give your reason + close warmly**:
  *"Maybe an hour. I want to finish this part, then I'll go. Okay, see you tomorrow — thanks for today."*
- 💡 **"You don't have to."** — permission, not a command. In English this is how one adult offers another adult a choice. Very common between colleagues.
- 💡 **"That's your call."** — 🎯 **perfect English for any decision.** Four syllables, no pressure, and it closes an argument without disagreement. *"It's your call"* is one of the most useful phrases in workplace English.
- 💡 **"Thanks for today."** — small, warm, and it ends the day properly. Far better than a silent wave. Say it every single day.
- 🎙️ Play `colleague-05` and answer. **Today: say "Thanks for today" to one real person before you leave.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** you already did this in **Day 4** (ending the stand-up) and **Day 9** (ending lunch). Notice it is the *same skill* — closing, not continuing. Most awkwardness in English is not bad grammar; it is **failing to close**. Everyone finds a way to stop. Your job is to learn one.
