# Day 12 · 📖 The Long Story — The Only List I've Ever Kept

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I don't shop with a list.** I used to think that was laziness, and for years
I bought the same six things every week: rice, eggs, tinned tomatoes, something
for lunch, washing-up liquid, and coffee. Then one evening I came home with
twenty-three pounds of shopping and no idea what half of it was.

The biscuits are the honest part of this story. They were by the till, which is
the most dangerous place in any supermarket, and they were the ones I'd seen in an
advertisement that week. I didn't walk past. I stopped, I looked at them, and I
made a decision, and I want to be honest that I made it deliberately.

The receipt was the proof. Four things I'd come in for, and then six items that
were entirely for me in that moment. My friend asked how much I spent and I said
twenty-three pounds, and she asked what for, and I said "I don't know," and then
I listed what I did know and the list got longer and neither of us said anything
for a while.

The reason this happened, and I only worked it out much later, is that shopping
is the only part of the week where nobody needs me to decide anything. At work I
choose carefully, because a wrong choice costs somebody else time. In a shop the
choice only costs £2.80 and nobody is waiting on me, and apparently I like that
far more than I expected.

So now I have a list, and the list works, and I still buy the biscuits. The
difference is that now I know they're coming, which means I don't feel surprised,
and I have started buying them in the middle of the shop where they're cheaper,
which means I'm still buying the biscuits, just with more dignity.

What I learned is that the boring answer is not "I have a list and I follow it."
The boring answer is "I have a list, and there's one thing on it I will always
break the list for."

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I used to think ___ was ___. Then ___. The reason is that ___. Now I ___.

Past perfect and past simple next to each other: *"I used to think… Then one evening I came home…"* That pairing is the backbone of a good spoken story.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **Depends what it is** · **which is silly** · **it's a real problem** · **do you take card?** · **can I pay separately?** · **the most dangerous place in any supermarket** · **they were by the till**

## ✏️ Speak for 60 seconds

*What is one thing you always buy, whatever your list says? Tell the story of the last time you did.*

- …you can use past perfect and past simple in the same story
- …you can explain a habit with *"the reason is that…"*
- …you can end with a turn of the story, not just a summary 🎯
