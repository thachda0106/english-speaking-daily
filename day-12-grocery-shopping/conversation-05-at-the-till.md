# Day 12 · Conversation 5 — At The Till

## 🎬 Situation
You're paying. This is the point where small errors become embarrassing, and where a few fixed phrases make you look comfortable instead of apologetic.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Sorry, I've got a card — do you take it here?

> **Staff:** Yes, that's fine.

> **You:** Great. Can I pay separately? I only have these two things.

> **Staff:** Sure, just scan them on their own.

> **You:** Right. And have you got a receipt?

> **Staff:** Here you are.

> **You:** Thank you. Sorry, do you know if the bag is included?

> **Staff:** It's five pence.

> **You:** Oh — here you are. Thank you very much.

> **Staff:** Have a good day.

> **You:** You too.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **do you take card here?** — how to ask about payment. British English: *"card"*, American: *"credit card"*
- **can I pay separately?** — splitting a bill. **Pay separately** is the phrase; *"pay apart"* is not English
- **just scan them on their own** — an instruction you'll hear a lot at a till
- **Have a good day** / **You too** — the standard closing. *"You too"* is short enough to say without thinking

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Sorry, do you take card? — Yes. — Can I pay separately? — Thank you very much.*

**"Thank you very much"** is the right register with a shop assistant — *"thanks a lot"* is fine with a friend, but slightly off when you're the customer.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask about payment and split a bill without hesitating
- …you can use *"do you take card?"*, *"can I pay separately?"*, *"you too"* as fixed phrases
- …you can end a shop interaction without a long goodbye 🎯
