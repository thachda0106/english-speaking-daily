# Day 12 · Conversation 3 — Fresh vs. Frozen

## 🎬 Situation
Someone asks which you prefer, and you have no strong opinion. This is small talk with a real opinion in it — and "it doesn't matter much" is a fine answer, as long as you add something.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** Do you buy fresh or frozen?

> **You:** Depends what it is. Frozen peas, always — they're better and cheaper.

> **Friend:** Really? I thought fresh was better.

> **You:** For some things, yeah. But peas get frozen within hours of picking them. Nobody's freezing them at home.

> **Friend:** That's true. What about tomatoes?

> **You:** Fresh. Frozen tomatoes are grim. But I never buy fresh — they go bad before I get home.

> **Friend:** So you don't like fresh tomatoes but you like frozen peas.

> **You:** That's exactly right. I'm not consistent about it.

> **Friend:** Fair enough. I'm the opposite — I buy everything fresh and it usually goes wrong.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **Depends what it is** — the perfect answer when you have no single preference. Better than *"I don't know"*
- **they get frozen within hours of picking them** — explaining a fact without saying *"I read somewhere"*
- **that's true** / **fair enough** — the two cheapest agreements in English. Both are genuine
- **I'm not consistent about it** — admitting a contradiction, which is friendlier than pretending you have a system

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Depends what it is. — [Reason]. — I'm not consistent about it.*

**"Depends what it is"** is a genuinely useful sentence for English conversation. It means *"there's no single rule"*, and it buys you time while you think of an actual reason.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can answer a preference question without a single fixed answer
- …you can give a reason with *"because"*, *"since"*, or a full sentence
- …you can disagree without being wrong 🎯
