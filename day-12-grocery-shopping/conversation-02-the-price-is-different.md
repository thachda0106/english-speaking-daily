# Day 12 · Conversation 2 — The Price Is Different

## 🎬 Situation
The label says one price. The till says another. This happens more than you'd think, and how you handle it decides whether you keep shopping there.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me, this was £2.50 on the shelf.

> **Staff:** Ah, it's just gone up to £2.80.

> **You:** Really? It was £2.50 yesterday.

> **Staff:** It might have. The price changes.

> **You:** Is there a cheaper one, or is that the price?

> **Staff:** There's the own-brand one at the end of the aisle — about £2.20, I think.

> **You:** I'll take that, then. Can you swap it, please?

> **Staff:** Sure. Do you want a receipt?

> **You:** No, it's fine. Thanks for checking.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **it's just gone up to** — a price change. *"just"* softens it, and it's how people say it
- **is there a cheaper one, or is that the price?** — one question that gets you the information and an alternative
- **can you swap it, please?** — asking for a different product. **Swap** is the right verb here
- **Do you want a receipt?** — standard. *"No, it's fine"* is a complete answer

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *This was ___ on the shelf. — It's gone up to ___. — Is there a cheaper one?*

⚠️ The phrase to avoid is *"you cheat me"*. It's aggressive and it puts the other person on defence. **Stating the two prices** gets the same result: *"It was £2.50 yesterday."* The facts do the arguing for you.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can query a price without sounding accusatory
- …you can ask for an alternative in one sentence
- …you can decline a receipt without apologising 🎯
