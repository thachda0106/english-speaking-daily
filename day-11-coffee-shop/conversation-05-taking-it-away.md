# Day 11 · Conversation 5 — Taking It Away

## 🎬 Situation
You're late, or you're on your way somewhere. You need the coffee to travel. Small thing, but it has its own vocabulary and a rule worth knowing.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi, can I get a coffee to take away, please?

> **Friend:** Sure. Which one?

> **You:** Americano, please. And… is it possible to have it in a cup rather than a plastic one? I've got a mug with me.

> **Friend:** Of course. Anything else?

> **You:** That's all, thank you. How long will it be?

> **Friend:** Two minutes.

> **You:** Great — I'll wait over there.

> **Friend:** Here you go. Careful, it's hot.

> **You:** Thanks. See you next week.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **to take away** — British and everyday. American English says *"to go"*
- **Is it possible to have it in a cup?** — a request framed as a question. Softer and more likely to be accepted than a statement
- **Anything else?** — the standard question before you leave. Answer *"That's all, thank you"*
- **Careful, it's hot.** — what people actually say. *"Be careful"* on its own sounds like a warning

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Can I get ___ to take away, please? — Which one? — ___, please.*

**"That's all, thank you"** closes every shop transaction in English. Learn it as one fixed phrase — trying to build it from parts sounds odd.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can order to take away without hesitating
- …you can ask for something environmentally friendly without sounding preachy
- …you can say *"that's all, thank you"* as one automatic phrase 🎯
