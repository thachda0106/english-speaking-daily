# Day 11 · 📖 The Long Story — The First Time I Ordered In English

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I hadn't ordered in English in years.** Not since school, and at school I was
bad at it. So when I started working in an office where everybody took coffee
breaks together, I did the thing I always do: I waited for someone else to order
and then I copied them. A colleague got a flat white. So I said "a coffee, please"
and hoped that was enough information.

It wasn't. The woman behind the counter asked me what size, and I said "yes."
She asked again, and this time I understood the question and said "small" in a
voice that came out much higher than I wanted. She asked how I wanted it, and I
said I didn't know the options. She told me — black, or with milk, latte, flat
white, cappuccino, americano — and I picked latte because it was the first word I
recognised.

Then I did the thing I regret most. She said "would you like anything to eat?"
and I panicked, because "no" felt rude and "yes" felt expensive, and what came out
of my mouth was: "I... only coffee. Thank you." She said "sure," and I have thought
about that pause maybe thirty times since.

The second week I ordered by myself. Latte, small, less sweet. The woman didn't
ask me anything. That was the whole victory: she had stopped needing to check
whether I understood. A month later I changed my order at the counter — I said
"actually, could you make that a flat white instead?" and she just said "sure" and
did it. Nobody in that café ever asked me why I ordered what I ordered, and I have
thought about that more than I should have, because it took me twenty-six years to
buy a coffee by myself and nobody thought it was strange.

What I learned, which I did not expect: the hard part was never the words. It was
the ten seconds before I spoke, the part where I decided whether I was allowed to
take up the person's time. Ten seconds later it was four words — *"a latte, please"* —
and I had been afraid of it for a decade.

I still order too slowly. I still say "I don't know the options" more than I need to.
But I order by myself now, and nobody minds, and the coffee is usually fine.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I hadn't done ___ in years. So I did the thing I always do: ___. — The hard part was never ___. It was ___.

**Past perfect** — *"I hadn't ordered in English in years"* — sets up something that happened before another past event. It is the tense that makes a story feel like it has a before and an after.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary
