# Day 11 · Conversation 4 — The Café You Keep Going Back To

## 🎬 Situation
You like a place and you go back. Someone asks how you found it. This is small talk with a shape — and it's the shape of a recommendation, which is a genuinely useful thing to be able to give.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** You come here a lot, don't you?

> **You:** Yeah, almost every week.

> **Friend:** What do you like about it?

> **You:** Honestly, it's not the coffee. It's that nobody minds me sitting here for two hours with my laptop.

> **Friend:** That's the nicest reason anyone's ever given for a café.

> **You:** And they don't play music, which I only noticed about a month ago.

> **Friend:** How did you find it in the first place?

> **You:** A colleague from my old job used to come here. He said, "try it, you'll like it." He was right.

> **Friend:** I should go there sometime.

> **You:** You should. Go in the morning — it gets busy after eleven.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I noticed** — past tense, and the natural word. Most people say *"I realised"*, which is stronger
- **How did you find it?** — how you discovered a place, not how you felt about it
- **He was right** — the shortest way to say someone was correct about a prediction
- **Go in the morning** — giving advice with a time attached, which sounds like real knowledge

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Honestly, it's not ___. It's that ___. — How did you find it?*

**"Honestly, it's not X, it's Y"** is a pattern worth stealing whole. It corrects an assumption politely, then answers the question you were *actually* asked. Writers use it constantly; so do good speakers.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can recommend a place with a reason and a time of day
- …you can use *"it's not X, it's Y"* without sounding defensive
- …you can say *"he was right"* instead of *"yes, exactly"* 🎯
