# Day 11 · Conversation 1 — Ordering For The First Time

## 🎬 Situation
You are at a café with a friend. You have never ordered here before and you don't know what the options are. This is the moment that decides whether you feel comfortable in the place or you quietly hope someone else orders for you.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi. Uh… can I get a coffee?

> **Friend:** Sure. What size?

> **You:** Small, please.

> **Friend:** And how do you want it?

> **You:** Sorry, I don't know the options.

> **Friend:** Black, or with milk? Latte, flat white, cappuccino, americano.

> **You:** Latte, please. And… could you make it less sweet?

> **Friend:** Sure. Anything to eat with that?

> **You:** Just the coffee, thank you.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I don't know the options.** — the natural way to admit you don't know something, and it's not embarrassing
- **How do you want it?** — how the person serving you asks about milk and sugar
- **Less sweet** — not sweet, but not sugar-free. A better word than *"no sugar,"* which usually means it tastes flat
- **Anything to eat with that?** — a café question. *"With that"* means "to go with it"

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Hi. Uh… can I get a ___? — Sure. ___? — [name] please.*

The hesitation is fine. Native speakers hesitate too — it sounds natural, not weak.

Say *"I don't know the options"* out loud. It's the sentence that saves you, and it takes practice before it feels natural.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can order one drink without switching to Vietnamese
- …you can ask one follow-up question (*"And how do you want it?"*)
- …you can say what you don't want — *"less sweet"*, *"no ice"* 🎯
