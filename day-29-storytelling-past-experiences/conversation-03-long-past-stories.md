# Day 29 · Conversation 3 — Long Past Stories

## 🎬 Situation
Some stories are about last week. Some are about when you were ten. Both use the same grammar, and both are useful — people love a story from your childhood.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** Do you have any stories from when you were a child?

> **You:** Yes. When I was ten, I broke my mother's phone. I dropped it in water.

> **Friend:** Oh no. What did you do?

> **You:** I put it in rice for two days. I read that rice takes out the water.

> **Friend:** Did it work?

> **You:** No. My mother was not happy. But she didn't say anything for a week.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`stories · child · ten · broke · mother · phone · dropped · water · put · rice · two days · read · takes out · work · no · happy · say anything · for a week · when I was`

## ✅ After you speak
- The childhood-story formula = **age + what you did + what you believed + what happened**:
  *"When I was ten, I broke my mother's phone. I dropped it in water, so I put it in rice for two days — I read that rice takes out the water."*
- 💡 **"When I was ten, …"** — 🎯 **the way to start any childhood story in English.** No need for *"When I was a child, a long time ago."* One clause is enough.
- 💡 **"I read that…"** — a beautiful softener for a bad idea. It says *I didn't know it was stupid; I read it somewhere.* Very honest, and a bit funny.
- 💡 **"But she didn't say anything for a week."** — the ending. It is funny because of the *for a week*, and it is true in every family in every language. Shared experience is what makes people laugh together.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-03` and answer. **Today: tell one story from when you were a child, in four sentences.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** a childhood story needs only four things: **an age, an action, a belief, and a consequence.** The belief is the funny part — what you thought would work. Children everywhere have this, which is why the stories always land.
