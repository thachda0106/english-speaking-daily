# Day 29 · Conversation 1 — Starting a Story

## 🎬 Situation
Every native speaker tells stories the same way, and it starts with one small phrase. This is the single biggest difference between you sounding fluent and sounding like you're translating.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** What did you do last weekend?

> **You:** Actually, something funny happened. I went to a coffee shop near my house.

> **Friend:** What happened?

> **You:** I ordered a coffee, and the man gave me tea instead. Same cup, same size.

> **Friend:** Oh no. Did you say anything?

> **You:** I said, "Thank you, but I asked for coffee." He looked at the cup and laughed.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`what did you do · last weekend · actually · something funny · happened · went · coffee shop · near · house · what happened · ordered · man · gave · tea instead · same cup · same size · said anything · thank you · asked for · looked · cup · laughed`

## ✅ After you speak
- The story-opener formula = **"Actually, something happened" + where + what**:
  *"Actually, something funny happened. I went to a coffee shop near my house."*
- 💡 **"Actually, something funny happened."** — 🎯 **the story opener.** Every native speaker uses a small phrase like this before the story. *"So the other day…"*, *"You know what happened?"*, *"Something weird happened."* You need one of these, because **the story is not the information — the phrase is the signal.** Without it, people do not know a story is coming.
- 💡 **"Something funny happened."** — *something* does not tell them what. That is the point. It makes them ask *"what happened?"* and now they are in your story with you.
- 💡 **"Same cup, same size."** — short repeated structure, very easy to say, and it makes the story funnier than a long explanation would.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-01` and answer. **Today: tell one true story in three sentences, starting with "Actually, something…"**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** a story has four beats: **signal → place → problem → result.** *"Actually…"* is the signal. *"At a coffee shop near my house"* is the place. *"He gave me tea"* is the problem. *"He laughed"* is the result. Four short pieces, and you already know every word.
