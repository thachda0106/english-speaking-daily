# Day 29 · Conversation 4 — Talking About People in Your Story

## 🎬 Situation
Stories have other people in them. Naming them and saying what they did is easy — but one wrong verb tense and the whole story goes strange.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** Who was with you when you moved to the new city?

> **You:** Only my family. My parents came with me, and my friend Ken stayed in our old city.

> **Friend:** That must have been hard. Did he visit?

> **You:** Yes, twice. The first time he stayed for a week, and we played football every day.

> **Friend:** And now he's here too?

> **You:** He is, actually. He started at my company last month. We see each other after work.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`who · with you · moved · new city · only · family · parents · came · friend · stayed · old city · hard · visit · yes · twice · first time · for a week · played · every day · now · here · started · company · last month · see · each other · after work`

## ✅ After you speak
- The people formula = **who + past action + a time + what you do now**:
  *"My friend Ken stayed in our old city. He is here now — he started at my company last month. We see each other after work."*
- 💡 **"That must have been hard."** — 🎯 **"must have" for a feeling you did not directly experience.** No past-tense verb needed. *"must have + adjective"* is the easiest way in English to guess someone's feeling, and it always sounds caring.
- 💡 **"We see each other after work."** — present tense for something that happens now and regularly. Native speakers use the present for habits. You did this in Day 15 too; now it is automatic.
- 💡 **"The first time…"** — useful for the first instance. *"The first time he came, we ate for three hours."*
- 🎙️ Play `friend-04` and answer. **Today: tell a story about another person, using "must have been."**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** remember **past for finished things, present for habits.** *"He stayed there for two years"* (finished). *"He lives there now"* (current). Mixing these up is the most common tense error in storytelling — and it changes nothing about whether people understand you, so do not stress about it in conversation.
