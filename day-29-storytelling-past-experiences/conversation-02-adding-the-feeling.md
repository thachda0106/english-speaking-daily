# Day 29 · Conversation 2 — Adding the Feeling

## 🎬 Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** What happened with the tea?

> **You:** I was a bit angry for a second. I didn't think he heard me right.

> **Friend:** What did you do then?

> **You:** I said it again, but slower. Then I started laughing too.

> **Friend:** That's a good ending. Were you really angry?

> **You:** For maybe three seconds. Then it was funny. I think he was not concentrating.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`what happened · bit angry · second · think · heard me right · did you do then · said again · slower · started laughing · good ending · really angry · maybe · three seconds · funny · concentrating · was not`

## ✅ After you speak
- The feeling formula = **feeling + time + what you did + what it became**:
  *"I was a bit angry for a second. Then it was funny — maybe three seconds."*
- 💡 **"A bit angry."** — *a bit* makes any feeling manageable. Nobody is *very angry* over tea. *"A bit"* is honest and small, so nobody worries.
- 💡 **"For a second."** / **"For three seconds."** — 🎯 **specific time is the whole trick.** *"I was angry"* is flat. *"I was angry for three seconds"* is a person with a memory. You already know every number you need: one, two, three, ten, an hour, a day.
- 💡 **"Then it was funny."** — past tense for a feeling. English allows it, and it sounds more honest than *"Then I found it funny."*
- 🎙️ Play `friend-02` and answer. **Today: describe a feeling using an exact amount of time.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** stories without feelings are reports. Stories with feelings are stories. You do not need the vocabulary of emotion — **a feeling plus a length of time is enough.** *"I was nervous for about an hour"* says everything.
