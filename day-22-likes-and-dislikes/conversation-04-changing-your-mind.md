# Day 22 · Conversation 4 — Changing Your Mind

## 🎬 Situation
You liked something two years ago and you don't now. Explaining that without sounding like you're claiming you were wrong for having liked it is genuinely hard.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** You used to be really into football, didn't you?

> **You:** Yeah, I was. Completely.

> **Friend:** What happened?

> **You:** Nothing happened. I just stopped caring, which is worse.

> **Friend:** Why do you think that is?

> **You:** Honestly? It got too big to be fun. Everyone had opinions about everything and I got tired of having one.

> **Friend:** That's a real reason.

> **You:** I don't hate it now, though. I'd watch a match if it was on.

> **Friend:** That's a healthy amount of interest.

> **You:** That's about right for me.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **you used to be really into ___** — **"used to"** for a past habit that's over. **"into"** is more enthusiastic than *"interested in"*
- **which is worse** — a short self-aware aside. It adds honesty to a flat statement
- **it got too big to be fun** — **"too + adjective + to + verb"** again, as in Day 16's *"too hot to be outside"*
- **that's a healthy amount of interest** — approving the *amount* rather than the thing. Very English

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I used to be really into ___. — Nothing happened. I just stopped ___, which is worse. — It got too ___ to be fun.*

⚠️ **"Used to" vs "would":** **"used to"** is for a habit that has stopped — *"I used to play every weekend"* (I don't now). **"would"** is for a repeated past action without implying it stopped — *"I would visit my grandmother every Sunday"* (in that period). Using *"would"* where you mean *"used to"* implies the habit might still be going.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can describe a habit you no longer have
- …you can use *"used to"* correctly, distinct from *"would"*
- …you can explain losing interest without blaming anyone 🎯
