# Day 22 · Conversation 3 — The Things You Like About A Place

## 🎬 Situation
Someone asks what you like about where you live. This is asked constantly, and the answer that gets a reaction is specific rather than complimentary.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** What do you like about living here?

> **You:** Honestly? The size of it. I can cycle across it in fifteen minutes.

> **Friend:** That's a good answer. Most people say the food.

> **You:** The food is fine. The food is everywhere.

> **Friend:** So what makes it work for you?

> **You:** I can do most things without a car, and I can still get out of it on a Sunday. That's the balance.

> **Friend:** That does sound right.

> **You:** It's not perfect. The buses stop at six, but the six o'clock train exists, so.

> **Friend:** That's the whole thing, then. The fallback.

> **You:** Exactly. You only need one plan to work.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **what do you like about living here?** — **"about living here"** is the phrase. *"What do you like here?"* is fine but vaguer
- **the size of it** — the way to say *"it's big"*. **"The size of it"** sounds natural; *"its size"* sounds like a spec sheet
- **the food is everywhere** — a flat dismissal with a reason attached. Efficient
- **you only need one plan to work** — the kind of sentence that makes people listen. Worth building deliberately

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Honestly? The size of it. — [why]. — I can do most things without a ___, and I can still get out of it on a ___.

⚠️ **"Honestly?" as an opener** signals that you're about to say something less conventional than the expected answer — and it makes whatever follows sound more honest. Native speakers use it constantly for exactly this.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can answer *"what do you like about living here?"* with something specific
- …you can use *"the size of it"* and *"the food is everywhere"*
- …you can finish with a principle: *"you only need one plan to work"* 🎯
