# Day 19 · Conversation 3 — At The Doctor's

## 🎬 Situation
You get an appointment and only have a few minutes. Most learners lose the important part because they answer questions in the wrong order. This is the order that works.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Doctor:** What seems to be the problem?

> **You:** I've had a headache for about four days, and it's worse in the morning.

> **Doctor:** Anything else?

> **You:** A temperature, mostly in the evening. And I'm more tired than I should be.

> **Doctor:** Right. Any cough or breathing problems?

> **You:** A dry cough, but no trouble breathing.

> **Doctor:** How long have you been feeling like this?

> **You:** Since Monday. But it got noticeably worse on Wednesday.

> **Doctor:** Right. Let's have a look.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **What seems to be the problem?** — the standard opener. *"I've got a problem"* would be far too dramatic
- **it's worse in the morning** — describing a pattern. This is the detail that helps most
- **more tired than I should be** — comparing to normal. Doctors understand this instantly
- **it got noticeably worse** — **"noticeably"** is the right adverb: worse in a way you'd mention to someone

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I've had ___ for about ___. — It's worse in the ___. — A ___, but no ___. — It got noticeably worse on ___.

⚠️ **Answer in this order: what → how long → when it's worse → what else.** Almost every doctor cuts you off if you start with everything at once. The person asking the questions wants one answer at a time.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can give a symptom summary in under twenty words
- …you can say *"it's worse in the ___"* without translating the pattern
- …you can say *"no trouble with ___"* to rule something out 🎯
