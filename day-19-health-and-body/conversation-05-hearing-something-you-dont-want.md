# Day 19 · Conversation 5 — Hearing Something You Don't Want

## 🎬 Situation
The doctor says something worrying. This happens to everyone, and the words for it are worth knowing in advance rather than in the moment.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Doctor:** I'd like to run a few tests. Nothing to worry about at this stage, but I'd rather check.

> **You:** Right. Okay. What happens next?

> **Doctor:** I'll write to you in about a week with the results.

> **You:** And if something's wrong?

> **Doctor:** Then I'll call you and we'd talk about what to do. But that's not where we are.

> **You:** Sorry, can I ask — how worried should I be?

> **Doctor:** Not very. It's routine. I don't want to alarm you.

> **You:** Right. Thank you. I'll try not to think about it.

> **Doctor:** That's usually the right approach.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **nothing to worry about at this stage** — **"at this stage"** is doing the reassuring work. It's not a promise
- **I'd rather check** — a doctor saying *precaution, not alarm*. A very good phrase to steal for your own work
- **that's not where we are** — saying *that's not the situation yet*. Repeated in different words, it means *relax*
- **how worried should I be?** — asking directly. Harder than it sounds, and completely legitimate

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *And if something's wrong? — How worried should I be? — [name]. What happens next?*

⚠️ **"How worried should I be?"** is a surprisingly hard sentence for a learner to say out loud, because *"worried"* is a big word. It is also the correct sentence, and doctors answer it plainly every day. **Practise it until it doesn't feel dramatic.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask *"how worried should I be?"* without apologising for asking
- …you can hear *"nothing to worry about"* and know what it means
- …you can ask *"what happens next?"* and get a real answer 🎯
