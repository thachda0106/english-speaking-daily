# Day 19 · Conversation 4 — Being Asked To Keep A Diary

## 🎬 Situation
The doctor wants you to track something for a week. This is a small skill, and it's also useful in work — you'll be asked to keep a log more often than you think.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Doctor:** I'd like you to keep a diary for a week. Note when the headaches happen, and what you were doing.

> **You:** Every headache, or just the bad ones?

> **Doctor:** All of them. Even the mild ones — that's usually where the pattern is.

> **You:** What should I write? Time, and what I ate?

> **Doctor:** Time, roughly what you were doing, and how bad it was. On a scale of one to ten.

> **You:** And if I forget a day?

> **Doctor:** Just leave it. Better than guessing.

> **You:** Okay. And when do you want to see me about it?

> **Doctor:** In a week. Same time.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **keep a diary** — record something regularly. **"keep a diary"**, not *"make a diary"*
- **even the mild ones** — insisting on the uninteresting data. Useful phrase for any kind of tracking
- **that's usually where the pattern is** — why the boring entries matter. Very useful at work too
- **better than guessing** — good reason for an honest answer. **"Better than"** compares two options without either being bad

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Every ___, or just the ___? — What should I write? ___ and roughly ___? — And if I ___?*

⚠️ **The clarifying questions are the skill here.** *"Every X, or just the Y?"* and *"What should I write?"* are how you turn a vague instruction into something you can actually do. That pattern works identically at work: *"Do you want this daily or weekly?"*

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask *"every ___, or just ___?"* to clarify an instruction
- …you can ask what fields to record before you start
- …you can say *"better than guessing"* as a reason 🎯
