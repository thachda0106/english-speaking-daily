# Day 20 · Conversation 5 — When It Goes Wrong

## 🎬 Situation
You gave notice, and the landlord isn't moving out. This is the hardest conversation in the day, and the one where English politeness actually works in your favour.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi, I've given notice and today is the last day, but the tenancy's still open.

> **Landlord:** There's still another viewing on Thursday.

> **You:** I'm sorry, but I need the place back today. I've already signed somewhere else for the first of next month.

> **Landlord:** I could let you stay a couple of weeks, rent-free?

> **You:** I appreciate that, but I can't — I've committed to the new place.

> **Landlord:** Then let's do it today. I'll cancel the viewing.

> **You:** Thank you. Could you confirm that in an email? I'll leave the keys with the neighbour as discussed.

> **Landlord:** That's fair. I'll do that this morning.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **the tenancy's still open** — **"tenancy"** is the word for the rental period; **"still open"** means not yet ended. Both are exact and legal-ish, which helps
- **I need the place back today** — **"I need ___ back"** is much stronger than *"I'd like the place back"*, and it doesn't sound rude
- **rent-free** — no rent, for a period. A very common UK rental word
- **could you confirm that in an email?** — getting a decision in writing. **"Confirm"** implies it was already agreed

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I've given notice and ___ is still open. — I need ___ back today. — I appreciate that, but I can't. — Could you confirm that in an email?*

⚠️ **"I appreciate that, but I can't"** is the single most useful sentence in a difficult negotiation. It acknowledges the offer properly — **so refusing it doesn't feel rude** — and still says no clearly. Say *"I'm afraid I can't"* if you want to soften further.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can say *"I need ___ back today"* firmly without aggression
- …you can refuse an offer with *"I appreciate that, but I can't"*
- …you can get a verbal agreement confirmed in writing 🎯
