# Day 20 · Conversation 1 — Looking At A Place

## 🎬 Situation
You're viewing a flat or a room. The vocabulary here is specific and practical, and it's the same in every English-speaking country.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Landlord:** Right, this is the front room. It's a double, so there's room for two beds or a bed and a desk.

> **You:** Is it furnished?

> **Landlord:** Yeah, there's a wardrobe and a desk. The bed stays.

> **You:** Okay. What about bills?

> **Landlord:** Rent is four-fifty a week, and bills are on top. Council tax is separate.

> **You:** Is that negotiable if I sign for longer?

> **Landlord:** For a year, I could do four-twenty.

> **You:** That's fair. Could I take it from the first of next month?

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **a double** — a room with room for two beds. **"single"** is for one. **"double"** is British and American, but British means the room, American often means two people sharing
- **a wardrobe** — the tall cupboard for clothes. **"closet"** is American, and used in British for a smaller one
- **bills are on top** — bills are *extra*, *in addition* to rent. **"on top"** is the key phrase
- **council tax is separate** — UK-specific, but the pattern — *X is separate* — is universal

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Is it ___? — [furnished, bills, what's included]. — If I sign for longer, is that negotiable? — Could I take it from ___?*

**"on top"** is worth learning precisely: *rent is £450 and bills are on top* means the total is higher than £450. Saying *"with bills"* can be heard as *"including bills"*, which is the opposite — **that distinction matters when you're signing anything.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask what's included without translating from a property listing
- …you can ask whether a price is negotiable
- …you can say *"bills are on top"* and be understood correctly 🎯
