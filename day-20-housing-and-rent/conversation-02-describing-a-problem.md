# Day 20 · Conversation 2 — Describing A Problem

## 🎬 Situation
Something is broken and you need it fixed or your money back. English has a very specific pattern for this, and being vague loses you the argument.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** The heating isn't working.

> **Landlord:** Right. It's been like that?

> **You:** Since Monday. And there's a damp patch on the wall by the window, which wasn't there before.

> **Landlord:** Let me have a look.

> **You:** Thanks. It's getting worse, actually — the wallpaper's started to lift.

> **Landlord:** Okay. I'll get someone in this week.

> **You:** Is that definite? I've got it in writing from the last time, and it took a fortnight.

> **Landlord:** I'll say Thursday. And I'll email you to confirm.

> **You:** That would be helpful, thank you.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **it's been like that?** — **present perfect continuous** asking *how long has this been going on*. More useful than *"when did it break?"*
- **there's a damp patch on the wall** — **"a damp patch"** is the exact phrase. *"wet spot"* is understood but vague
- **the wallpaper's started to lift** — **"has started to"** marks the point of change. Precise and professional
- **Is that definite?** — one word that escalates a complaint. Much better than *"that's not very definite"*

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *[Thing] isn't working. — It's been ___ since ___. — And there's a ___ by the ___, which wasn't there before. — Is that definite?*

⚠️ **"It's been like that" is the sentence that gets you a fix.** It's *present perfect continuous*, and it asks about duration rather than blame — so the other person has to answer with a timeline, which is what forces them to act.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can say how long something has been broken
- …you can describe a physical problem precisely enough to be understood
- …you can press for a date with *"is that definite?"* 🎯
