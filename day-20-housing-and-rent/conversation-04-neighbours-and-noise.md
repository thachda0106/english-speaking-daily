# Day 20 · Conversation 4 — Neighbours And Noise

## 🎬 Situation
Something is bothering you and the person responsible is not your landlord. English has a whole register for this: direct but not aggressive.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Neighbour:** Hi — sorry, did I hear music last night? It was a bit loud.

> **You:** Yeah, sorry about that. I didn't realise how far it carried.

> **Neighbour:** It's fine. I just have a really early start.

> **You:** I get it. I'll turn it down after eleven — is that okay?

> **Neighbour:** Yeah, that works. And if it's ever a problem, just knock. I'd rather say something than text.

> **You:** Same. I'll knock tonight, before it happens again.

> **Neighbour:** That works. Thanks for being reasonable about it.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **did I hear music last night?** — the softest possible way to raise it. A question, not an accusation
- **I didn't realise how far it carried** — **"how far it carries"** is the useful part. It describes the sound, not your character
- **I just have a really early start** — explaining without complaining. **"I just…"** makes the other person the reason
- **that's okay / that works** — two different agreements. **"Okay"** accepts the request; **"that works"** accepts the *timing*

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Did I hear ___? — I didn't realise how far it ___. — I'll ___ after eleven, is that okay? — If it's ever a problem, just knock.*

⚠️ **The two-step structure is what works:** take responsibility in one clause **without a full apology**, then offer a specific change. *"Sorry, I was being a idiot"* reads as over-apologising; *"I didn't realise how far it carried"* reads as **a competent neighbour who noticed and fixed it.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can raise a noise complaint without escalating
- …you can take responsibility without grovelling
- …you can offer a specific change with a time attached 🎯
