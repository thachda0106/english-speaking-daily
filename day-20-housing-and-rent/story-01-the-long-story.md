# Day 20 · 📖 The Long Story — The Flat I Didn't Check

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I moved into a flat in about four minutes.** I want to be clear that this is
what actually happened, because it sounds like exaggeration and it isn't. I had
been in the country for eleven days, I had a job starting on the first, I needed
to be in the flat on the first, and so when I found one that was affordable and
available immediately, I signed for it.

The viewing took four minutes because there was nobody there to have one with. I
opened the wardrobe, I looked at the window, I pressed the heating button, and I
said yes. I didn't take a photograph of anything. I didn't turn the taps on. I
did not ask what the council tax was, because I didn't yet know what a council
tax was.

The damp was visible in the first week. Not a mark, not a smell — the wallpaper
in the corner by the window had started to lift, and by the second week it was
coming away in a strip you could hold. I told myself it was the weather. It was
October. That was not a good enough answer and I knew it at the time, which is
the part I keep coming back to.

**The heating was the worse problem, and it was the one that made me contact the
landlord.** It had not worked since the day I moved in. The landlord came on the
following Tuesday — eight days — and told me, on the doorstep, that it had
probably been turned off at the valve because the last tenant had done that
sometimes. He turned it back on. That was it. Eight days of no hot water for one
turn of a valve that takes ten seconds.

What makes this worse is that the money came back. It did, eventually, and the
landlord was, on the whole, reasonable. But not having any record of the state of
the flat meant every conversation started with me trying to remember. I had no
photographs, no inventory, no date. So I said *"I think it was fine when I moved
in,"* which is exactly the sentence you say when you know you should have brought
evidence.

What I learned is that four minutes of checking is not enough, and that the
things you skip when you are in a hurry are exactly the things you will need when
you are not in a hurry any more. Photographs take four minutes. Turning on the
taps takes four minutes. Asking what is included takes four minutes. I did all
three properly the next time, and it took four minutes, and it prevented about six
weeks of argument.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I moved into a ___ in about four minutes. The ___ was the worse problem. What makes this worse is that ___. What I learned is that ___.

Past perfect for what happened before the damage — *"It had not worked since the day I moved in"* — and simple past for the sequence of events. Keeping those two straight is what makes a complaint sound factual instead of emotional.
