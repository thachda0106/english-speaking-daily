# Day 20 · Conversation 3 — The Deposit And The Paperwork

## 🎬 Situation
Money and documents are involved. This conversation happens exactly once and involves a lot of money, so precision matters more than warmth here.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Landlord:** So the deposit would be five weeks' rent, held in the deposit protection scheme.

> **You:** And that comes back to me at the end?

> **Landlord:** Yeah, minus any deductions. There's an inventory you sign on day one.

> **You:** What kind of deductions? I don't want to argue in six months about a scratch.

> **Landlord:** Fair enough. We'd go through it together and put it in writing.

> **You:** Can I have a copy of the inventory before I sign?

> **Landlord:** Absolutely. I'll email it tonight.

> **You:** That's all I needed. When would you need a decision by?

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **five weeks' rent** — **the apostrophe matters**: *five weeks' rent* is "the rent for five weeks". Without it, *"five weeks rent"* is heard but wrong in writing
- **deductions** — money taken off the deposit. The exact word agencies use
- **an inventory** — the list of the state of the property, room by room. **This word has no direct equivalent** — knowing it is the point
- **can I have a copy before I sign?** — the single most valuable question in any rental conversation

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *[What's the deposit?] — What kind of ___? — Can I have a copy of ___ before I sign? — When would you need a decision by?*

⚠️ **"Can I have a copy of the inventory before I sign?"** protects the whole relationship. Without a signed inventory, the landlord can claim anything at the end — and most disputes about deposits are really disputes about whether damage existed before you arrived. **Always get it in writing before you sign, never after.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask exactly what a deposit covers
- …you can request the inventory before signing
- …you can ask for a deadline without sounding rushed 🎯
