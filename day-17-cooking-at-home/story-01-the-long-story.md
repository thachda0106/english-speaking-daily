# Day 17 · 📖 The Long Story — The Only Dish I Can Make

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I can cook exactly one thing.** Not one thing well — one thing. There is a
curry, a friend taught it to me in about fifteen minutes about three years ago,
and it is the only thing I have made since. I am not proud of this and I have
stopped pretending to be.

The way it happened is that cooking, for someone who doesn't cook, is mostly
friction. You have to decide what to eat, which means thinking. You have to find
the thing, which means a cupboard. You have to decide about the thing, which
means more thinking. And then at the end there's cleaning, which has no
reward at all, which is exactly the part I can't get past. So I stopped cooking
entirely, for about a year, and I ate the same four things on rotation until a
friend stayed over and refused to eat takeaway for the fourth weekend running.

The curry was her idea, and it was built specifically for my weaknesses. You
chop everything and put it in a pan, and it can't go badly. She said, and I
remembered it for years, "if you can't ruin it, you'll cook it again." That
sentence is the reason I cook at all. Not because it's good food — it's decent,
not exceptional — but because the difficulty was the only obstacle, and she'd
removed the obstacle.

What I did not expect was that I'd get bored of it. That took about four months.
I was bored, and I noticed I was bored, and I kept making it anyway, and
somewhere in there I stopped finding it strange. There's a word for that in
English that I didn't have before and now use constantly, which is "routine" —
and my friend said something on one of those evenings that I've never forgotten.
She said: "that's not a contradiction, that's a routine."

That reframed it. I had been treating the boredom as evidence that I'd failed at
cooking, when actually it was just what happens when you do the same thing
enough. That's not a cooking insight, that's an insight about anything you repeat
at work, and I have thought about it more in relation to code review than to
dinner.

So now I say it out loud before anyone asks. I can make one dish, I've made it a
few hundred times, and if you come over on a Tuesday you are eating that. People
find it funny. They also stop suggesting restaurants, which was honestly half
the point.

What I learned is that a small reliable thing beats an ambitious thing you'll
abandon. Not because of the food — because of the cleaning.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I can cook exactly one thing. The way it happened is that ___. — What I did not expect was that ___. — What I learned is that ___.

Passive voice for the mechanism — *"Cooking is mostly friction"* — keeps you from sounding like you're complaining about yourself. Use it when a sentence is about the situation, not your character.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary
