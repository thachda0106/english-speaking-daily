# Day 17 · Conversation 2 — It Failed

## 🎬 Situation
You cooked something and it's bad. English gives you four or five different ways to say that, and picking the right one is a social skill.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** How's it going? You look worried.

> **You:** I think I ruined it.

> **Friend:** What happened?

> **You:** I used the tinned tomatoes instead of fresh, and I put them in too early.

> **Friend:** Ah. That'll have made them mushy.

> **You:** Yeah. It's edible but it's not good.

> **Friend:** Do you want to start again?

> **You:** Honestly? No. I'll eat it. It's not the worst thing I've made.

> **Friend:** That's the right answer. Nobody's died.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I think I ruined it** — more accurate than *"it's bad"*. **"Think"** leaves room for it being fixable
- **It's edible but it's not good** — the calmest possible way to admit failure. Very useful, because it's true
- **Do you want to start again?** — a practical offer, not *"are you okay?"*
- **Nobody's died** — dark, very funny, and completely normal among friends

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I think I ruined it. — [what you did wrong]. — It's edible but it's not good. — I'll eat it.*

⚠️ **Four ways to say it failed, in order of honesty:**
| Phrase | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| *It's not great* | too polite — a lie |
| *It's a bit disappointing* | you're pretending to be diplomatic |
| *It's edible but it's not good* | honest, self-aware |
| *I ruined it* | you know exactly what happened |

**"It's edible but it's not good"** is the sweet spot: it takes responsibility without apologising three times.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can name what went wrong using cooking verbs
- …you can admit failure without a long apology
- …you can accept a bad result with *"it's edible but it's not good"* 🎯
