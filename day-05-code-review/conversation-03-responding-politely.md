# Day 5 · Conversation 3 — Responding Politely to a Disagreement

## 🎬 Situation
Now the hard one: the reviewer disagrees with your design choice. Your data model vs. their simpler idea. The skill: agree with the goal, check the team's pattern, follow it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Reviewer:** One question — why did you choose a new table for this instead of adding a column?

> **You:** Good question. I thought a new table is cleaner because the data has a different life cycle.

> **Reviewer:** Hmm, I see your point, but a column would be simpler.

> **You:** That's fair. Could I check how we usually handle this in the codebase? I don't want to go against the team's pattern.

> **Reviewer:** Sounds good. If the pattern is there, follow it.

> **You:** Will do. Thanks for pushing me to think about it.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`question · chose · new · table · instead · column · cleaner · data · different · point · simpler · fair · check · usually · handle · codebase · against · pattern · follow · pushing · think`

## ✅ After you speak
- The polite-disagreement formula = **accept + check the pattern + follow it**:
  *"That's fair. Let me check how we usually do this. I don't want to go against the team's pattern."*
- 💡 **"I see your point, but…"** — the respectful way to compare your idea with theirs. You acknowledge them before you explain.
- 💡 **"push me to think"** — meta-praise: thank them for making you think harder. *"Thanks for pushing me to think about it."*
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** "life cycle" = the life of the data: when it's born, used, and gone. A column lives and dies with the row; a separate table has its own life.
