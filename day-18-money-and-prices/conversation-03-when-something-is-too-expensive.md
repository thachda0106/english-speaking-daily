# Day 18 · Conversation 3 — When Something Is Too Expensive

## 🎬 Situation
You want something and you can't justify it. Saying no to yourself out loud is harder than it sounds, and English has honest ways to do it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** What are you looking at?

> **You:** This coat. Which is ridiculous, because I have four coats.

> **Friend:** That's what I said about the shoes last month.

> **You:** How did you talk yourself out of it?

> **Friend:** I didn't. I bought them.

> **You:** Well, that's honest.

> **Friend:** Yeah, it's a bit of a trap. You can always afford it, you just can't afford it twice.

> **You:** That's going on the receipt.

> **Friend:** Steal it. I'll put it on a card for you.

> **You:** No — I'll just close the shop and go home.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **that's ridiculous** — calling your own behaviour absurd. Funny rather than self-pitying
- **talk yourself out of it** — deciding **not** to buy something. *"out of"* not *"off"*
- **I didn't** — the shortest possible answer, and here it's the most honest one
- **You can always afford it, you just can't afford it twice** — a genuinely good line. Notice the repetition of structure

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *This is ridiculous, because I already have ___. — I could afford it. I just couldn't afford it twice.*

⚠️ **A genuinely useful pattern:** *"I can afford it, but…"* followed by the real reason. It separates **money** from **decision**. Most people say *"I can't afford it"* when they mean *"I shouldn't buy it"* — and those are different problems with different answers.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can talk yourself out of a purchase out loud
- …you can say *"I can afford it, but…"* and finish the sentence honestly
- …you can joke about your own bad decisions without self-pity 🎯
