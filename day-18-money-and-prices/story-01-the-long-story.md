# Day 18 · 📖 The Long Story — The Two Months I Didn't Look

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I did not look at my bank account for about two months.** Not because I was
broke — I was fine, boringly fine — but because I had a feeling about it, and
looking would either confirm the feeling or ruin it. Both outcomes were worse
than not knowing.

So I didn't look. And then I did something considerably stupider, which was to
track my spending in a supermarket instead, on my phone, standing next to the
frozen pizza, adding things up with one thumb while a woman tried to get past me
with a trolley.

The numbers were fine, by the way. That was almost the worst part. I was not
broke. I had simply been telling myself I was careful, and I was careful about
entirely the wrong things, and no amount of arithmetic was going to fix that.

What I actually found was four subscriptions I had completely forgotten about,
taking roughly thirty pounds a month out of an account I thought I knew inside
out. That was the useful finding, and I felt slightly foolish about it. The
uncomfortable finding was this: I don't make bad decisions about money in one
go. I make four hundred small ones, spread across a year, every single one of
them completely reasonable on the day.

**The second coffee is where I actually drew the line.** I have a fixed amount of
money coming in, I have moved the limit down to one coffee a day, and I would
like to keep it to one coffee a day. A friend said that was a bit sad, and I said
yes, it is a bit sad, and I am keeping it anyway. It was the first time in my
life I let a small decision be a principle instead of a preference.

It sounds like I am describing being careful with money. I am not. I am
describing the discovery that your finances are a set of habits that feel
exactly like luck, right up until the month you write them down.

What I learned is that the thing I was avoiding was never the number. It was the
realisation that I don't know what I'm doing — and that not knowing is far more
comfortable than knowing and still doing nothing about it.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I did not ___ for about ___. Not because I was ___ — I was ___. — What I actually found was ___. What I learned is that ___.*

**Em dashes** are the writer's pause. Spoken aloud, treat each one as a full stop and begin a new sentence. It sounds natural, and it stops you running out of breath mid-story.
