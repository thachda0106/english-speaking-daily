# Day 18 · Conversation 4 — When Someone Offers To Pay

## 🎬 Situation
Someone offers to pay and you need to refuse without making it weird. English makes this easy once you know that the first refusal is a formality, not a decision.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** I'll get this.

> **You:** Really? That's very kind of you.

> **Friend:** It's alright.

> **You:** Are you sure? We can split it.

> **Friend:** Honestly, it's my treat.

> **You:** Okay. Thank you — I'll get the next one.

> **Friend:** Fine by me.

> **You:** I mean it, though. You don't have to do that.

> **Friend:** I know. That's why I'm offering.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **that's very kind of you** — the standard reply. **"of you"** is required; *"that's very kind"* sounds slightly cold on its own
- **it's my treat** — **"my treat"** means this one's on me. Very useful and rarely taught
- **I'll get the next one** — the answer that makes accepting easy. You're not accepting a favour, you're starting a sequence
- **I mean it** — insisting that you're sincere. Used when someone thinks you're being polite

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Really? That's very kind of you. — Are you sure? We can split it. — Okay. Thank you — I'll get the next one.*

⚠️ **The rule English speakers follow without discussing it:** you **refuse once**, you **offer an alternative**, and then you **accept**. Refusing three times is uncomfortable for both sides — it forces them to insist harder. *"Are you sure? We can split it"* is the ideal middle move: it's a genuine offer, so you're not just performing politeness.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can refuse an offer once and then accept it comfortably
- …you can say *"I'll get the next one"* to close the exchange
- …you can use *"it's my treat"* for your own offer 🎯
