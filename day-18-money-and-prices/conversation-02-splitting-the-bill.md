# Day 18 · Conversation 2 — Splitting The Bill

## 🎬 Situation
You went out with people and someone reaches for the bill. This is a social moment with an exact phrase, and getting it wrong is expensive.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** I'll get this one, then.

> **You:** No, no — let's split it.

> **Friend:** Are you sure? I got dinner last time.

> **You:** It was a small thing last time. This one's different.

> **Friend:** Okay. What did you have?

> **You:** Just the pasta and a drink.

> **Friend:** Right, mine's about twelve more.

> **You:** That's fine. Can we do it by phone, or is it easier to just pay separately?

> **Friend:** Separately is easier. Then nobody has to do sums.

> **You:** Perfect. That's what I'll do then.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **let's split it** — the standard phrase. **"split the bill"** is British, **"split the check"** American; *"split it"* works everywhere
- **It was a small thing last time** — the honest reason for insisting, without *"because I insist"*
- **by phone** — transferring money. *"Can we do it by phone?"* is exactly right
- **nobody has to do sums** — British and informal. **"Sums"** means arithmetic; it's warmer than *"does the calculation"*

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *No, no — let's split it. — It was a small thing last time. — Can we do it by phone, or is it easier to pay separately?*

**"Let's split it"** is the phrase that ends a debate. Note you often need a **double negative to refuse politely**: *"No, no —"*. One *"no"* can sound like the start of a longer argument; two makes it clear the matter is closed and you're being warm about it.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can insist on splitting with *"no, no — let's split it"*
- …you can give a real reason for insisting
- …you can offer two payment options in one sentence 🎯
