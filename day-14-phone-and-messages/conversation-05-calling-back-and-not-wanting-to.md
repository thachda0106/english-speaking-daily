# Day 14 · Conversation 5 — Calling Back, And Not Wanting To

## 🎬 Situation
Someone rang. You didn't pick up. Now you have to ring back, and you'd rather not. There's a way to do this that's honest without being apologetic.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi, it's Thach — I got your voicemail earlier. Sorry, I was in a meeting.

> **Them:** No worries, I know how it is.

> **You:** How's your week going?

> **Them:** Busy. Not bad. How about you?

> **You:** Pretty full, honestly. Is there a good time to actually talk about this?

> **Them:** Yeah, tomorrow's better. I've got a gap around eleven.

> **You:** That works — shall I ring you then?

> **Them:** Yeah, go for it. Is it urgent?

> **You:** Not really, no. It's the report from last week — it just needs eyes on it.

> **Them:** Perfect. Tomorrow at eleven then.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I got your voicemail** — **"got"** not *"received"*. Voicemail is informal; formal email would be *"I received your message"*
- **How's your week going?** — buying thirty seconds before business. Notice how it's about the week, not the call
- **Is there a good time to actually talk about this?** — **"actually"** carries the whole meaning: *I'd rather not do this over the phone right now*
- **Is it urgent?** — asking someone to set your priority. Perfectly normal

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I got your voicemail — sorry, I was in a meeting. — Is there a good time to actually talk about this? — Is it urgent? — Not really, it just needs eyes on it.*

**"Is there a good time to actually talk about this?"** is the single most useful sentence in this conversation. It moves a conversation off the phone and onto a time you chose — without refusing anything.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can return a missed call without over-explaining
- …you can buy a moment of small talk with *"how's your week going?"*
- …you can ask *"is there a good time to actually talk about this?"* and mean it 🎯
