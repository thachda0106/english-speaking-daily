# Day 14 · Conversation 1 — You Can't Hear Them

## 🎬 Situation
The line is bad and you have missed half of what they said. This happens on every call in every country. In English it has fixed phrases, and using one is completely normal.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Sorry, I can't hear you — could you repeat that?

> **Them:** I said we'd move the deadline to Friday.

> **You:** Sorry, you're breaking up a bit. Are you on mobile?

> **Them:** Yeah, I'm in the car.

> **You:** That's probably it. Can you move to somewhere quieter?

> **Them:** Give me a second.

> **You:** Sure, take your time.

> **Them:** Right — so, Friday. Is that okay for you?

> **You:** Friday works. Thanks for bearing with me.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **you're breaking up a bit** — the natural way to say the connection is cutting out. Not *"your voice is bad"*
- **Can you move to somewhere quieter?** — asking someone to change where they are, politely, without an explanation
- **Give me a second** — *"second"* singular is the natural form here. *"Give me some seconds"* is not English
- **Thanks for bearing with me** — for making someone patient while you sort something out. Very common on calls

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Sorry, I can't hear you — could you repeat that? — You're breaking up a bit. Can you move somewhere quieter?*

**Never apologise more than once.** *"Sorry, I can't hear you"* is complete politeness. Adding *"sorry, sorry"* makes you sound anxious rather than polite.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask someone to repeat without apologising twice
- …you can say *"you're breaking up"* and be understood immediately
- …you can request a quieter spot as a request, not an explanation 🎯
