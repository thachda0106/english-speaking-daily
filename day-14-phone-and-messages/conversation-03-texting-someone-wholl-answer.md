# Day 14 · Conversation 3 — Texting Someone Who'll Answer

## 🎬 Situation
You text a friend or a colleague and they reply. A text conversation moves much faster than speech, which means one bad line can end it. Knowing the conventions is the whole skill.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** You around later?

> **Them:** Maybe — depends how late you mean.

> **You:** Seven-ish?

> **Them:** That works. What about it?

> **You:** Thought we could get food. The place near the office, not the one by the station.

> **Them:** Yeah okay. My phone's been rubbish all day though, so if I go quiet I've probably just lost signal.

> **You:** Same, mine's on about four percent. Just tell me when you're leaving.

> **Them:** Will do.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **You around later?** — four words that open an entire social invitation. **"around"** is the key word; *"are you around"* means available
- **depends how late you mean** — pushing back lightly without saying no. Extremely natural
- **That works.** — agreeing. Very useful; warmer than *"ok"* and firmer than *"sure!"*
- **What about it?** — *"why?"* but much less blunt

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *You around later? — [when]. — That works. What about it? — Thought we could ___.*

**"That works"** is the phrase to steal here. English has about fifteen ways to agree, and *"That works"* is the one that sounds relaxed rather than formal or enthusiastic.

⚠️ Note what's **not** there: no *"Dear…"*, no full sentences, no *"I was wondering if…"*. Texting English is a genuinely different register.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can open with *"You around later?"* and get an answer
- …you can say *"that works"* as your default agreement
- …you can keep a text thread alive without over-typing 🎯
