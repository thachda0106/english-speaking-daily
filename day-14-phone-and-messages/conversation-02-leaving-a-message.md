# Day 14 · Conversation 2 — Leaving A Message

## 🎬 Situation
Someone isn't answering. You need to leave a message that is useful — clear about who you are, why you're calling, and what you need.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Hi, it's Thach. Is Duy there?

> **Answerphone:** Sorry, he's not in at the moment. Leave a message.

> **You:** Yeah, could you tell him it's Thach from the payments team?

> **Answerphone:** Sure.

> **You:** It's about the release on Thursday — the migration script needs a quick check before it goes out.

> **Answerphone:** Right.

> **You:** And if he's back after six, could he text me? I'm off at half five.

> **Answerphone:** Sure, I'll pass that on.

> **You:** Thanks very much.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **Is ___ there?** — the standard opener. Not *"are you there?"*, which sounds odd
- **could you tell him it's ___?** — asking the machine to pass on your name. Very routine, not apologetic
- **it's about** — introducing the topic of a message. Two of the most useful words in English for opening with a reason
- **could he text me?** — asking for a text rather than a call. **"Text me"** is standard; *"message me"* is odd on a phone

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Hi, it's ___. Is ___ there? — Could you tell him it's ___ from ___? — It's about ___. Could he text me?*

Four lines and you've left a message someone can actually act on. The habit that makes it natural: **name → topic → what you need.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can leave a complete message in under fifteen seconds
- …you can open with *"could you tell him…"* without sounding apologetic
- …you can end on a clear request 🎯
