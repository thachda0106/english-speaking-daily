# Day 14 · Conversation 4 — Being Abbreviated At

## 🎬 Situation
You've known someone for years, and now every message is four letters long. This is normal in English, and once you know it you'll use it too.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** hey, u ok? that was the meeting w the other team

> **Them:** yeah survived lol. they went on forever

> **You:** no kidding. did the budget thing come up

> **Them:** yep. pushed back tmrw apparently

> **You:** on what? i thought it was signed off already

> **Them:** yeah that's the bit i dont get. want me to dig in or leave it

> **You:** can u ask them what changed? might be something we missed

> **Them:** will do. im out anyway, ttyl

> **You:** ta

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **u / ur / ur** — texting register for *you / your / you're*. Never in speech, never at work first thing
- **no kidding** — *"you're telling me"*. Said when something is absurd
- **the budget thing** — **"the ___ thing"** is a work phrase: naming a problem by its category instead of its details
- **ttyl / ta** — *"talk to you later"* / *"thanks"*. Three and two letters. Extremely normal

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *[thing] come up? — Yeah. Want me to dig in or leave it? — Can you ask them what changed?*

**"Want me to dig in or leave it?"** is the whole point of this conversation. It's how a competent person offers work in a text — two options, no pressure. Phrase it exactly like that and you'll sound senior.

⚠️ **Abbreviation is a register, not a skill.** It works with people who text that way. Using it in a first message to someone senior, or in any email, reads as overfamiliar.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can read a heavily abbreviated message without guessing
- …you can offer work with *"want me to dig in or leave it?"*
- …you can match someone's register without over-copying it 🎯
