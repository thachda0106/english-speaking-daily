# Day 14 · 📖 The Long Story — The Phone Call I Kept Putting Off

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**There is a phone call I have been putting off for about three weeks.** It is
with a colleague I like, and the call itself takes four minutes, and I have
replied to his messages the whole time. Just not the call.

The first reason was the phone itself. My English is worse on the phone than it
is in person, and I could hear it, and so could he, and I did not want him to
hear it. The second reason, which took me longer to admit, is that I did not
know how to end the call. Texting is easy — you write, you send, you stop. There
is no moment where you have to say goodbye.

So I emailed instead. I have written that email three times now. The first two
versions were far too long, because I was trying to explain why I was not
calling, which made the not-calling worse. The third one I finished in four lines
and then sat looking at it for ten minutes before I hit send.

What finally worked was something a friend told me about her own work phone
calls, and I think about it often. She said the reason phone calls are hard is
that we treat them as the whole conversation, when really a phone call is a small
piece of a conversation that mostly happens on a screen. Once I understood that,
the call stopped feeling like a test. It was just the awkward part.

So I rang him this week. It took two minutes and forty seconds. I said sorry for
the delay, he said no worries, and then we got through the thing I needed to say,
which was that I could not make the Thursday date. He said that was completely
fine. That was the entire call, and I had spent three weeks and two drafts of
email being anxious about it.

What I learned is that the dread was never about the four minutes. It was about
being unable to end something. English gives you a way out of that which I wish
I had known months ago: "is there a good time to actually talk about this?" It
sounds polite and reasonable, and what it actually does is move the whole
conversation into the future, where you control the timing and you can think
before you speak.

The thing I should have known earlier is that he did not care at all. He had
forgotten about it twice. It was me, entirely, the whole time.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *There is a ___ I have been putting off for ___. The first reason was ___. The second reason, which took me longer to admit, is that ___. What I learned is that ___.

Past perfect for the situation before the story — *"I have been putting off"* — then past simple for what happened. The second reason pattern is worth copying: it shows you are about to say something harder than you expected.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I got your voicemail** · **you're breaking up** · **that works** · **no worries** · **give me a second** · **is there a good time to actually talk about this?** · **want me to dig in or leave it?** · **no kidding**
