# Day 23 · Conversation 2 — Half Agreeing

## 🎬 Situation
You agree with the direction but not the timing, or the idea but not the method. This is the most common real disagreement, and English has an exact structure for it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** We should launch all at once. It'll look more confident.

> **You:** I agree with the confidence. I'm not sure about all at once.

> **Friend:** Why?

> **You:** Because we'll learn things in the first week, and we'd have to undo them in public.

> **Friend:** Hmm. So what are you suggesting?

> **You:** Launch with the two things that work, and keep the rest behind a flag for a month.

> **Friend:** That sounds more complicated.

> **You:** It is a bit. But it's easier to fix quietly than loudly.

> **Friend:** Okay, that makes sense. Let's do that.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I agree with the ___, but I'm not sure about the ___** — the exact shape of partial agreement. Agree with the goal, object to the method
- **we'd have to undo them in public** — **"in public"** vs *"privately"*. That single contrast carries the argument
- **it is a bit, but** — **"but"** at the end of a short clause. Accepting a cost and then justifying it
- **that makes sense** — genuine agreement with someone's reasoning. Better than *"you're right"*, which is blunter

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I agree with the ___, but I'm not sure about the ___. Because ___. — What are you suggesting? — It is a bit, but ___.*

⚠️ **Separating the goal from the method is the whole skill.** *"I agree with X but not Y"* lets the other person keep their goal, so you're not fighting them — you're adjusting the route. Without that split, your objection reads as a rejection of the idea itself.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can agree with part of a proposal and object to the rest
- …you can explain the objection in one sentence
- …you can accept a cost and justify it: *"it is a bit, but…"* 🎯
