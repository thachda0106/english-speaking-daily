# Day 23 · Conversation 3 — Saying No Without Winning

## 🎬 Situation
You're being asked to do something you don't want to. English lets you decline without apologising twice, which is harder than it sounds.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** Could you review this today? I know it's short.

> **You:** I can't today.

> **Friend:** Why? I thought it was only twenty minutes.

> **You:** It is. It's just not twenty minutes I have today.

> **Friend:** Okay. When could you?

> **You:** Tomorrow morning, first thing. It'll be done properly then.

> **Friend:** That works better than today, actually. Thank you.

> **You:** No problem.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I can't today** — the bare no. No reason attached, which is the point: **you don't owe one**
- **it's just not twenty minutes I have today** — **"it's just not ___ I have"**. Reframing the quantity, not the task. Very calm and very firm
- **when could you?** — the question that turns a refusal into a schedule
- **that works better than today** — them saying your no was helpful. It usually is

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I can't today. — It's just not ___ I have today. — I could do it ___.*

⚠️ **"I can't" vs "I won't":** *"I can't"* is a statement about your circumstances, which nobody can argue with. *"I won't"* sounds like a decision about the task, which they *will* argue with. **"I can't today"** keeps the conversation on timing instead of willingness.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can say no in two words
- …you can offer a specific alternative time
- …you can refuse without inventing an excuse 🎯
