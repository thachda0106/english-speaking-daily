# Day 23 · Conversation 5 — When You Know You're Right

## 🎬 Situation
You're sure the other person is wrong. English lets you say so with complete confidence and without causing offence — and the difference is entirely in the phrasing.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** I think we should rewrite it from scratch. The structure's wrong.

> **You:** The structure's fine. The copy's the problem.

> **Friend:** Really? I've looked at it three times.

> **You:** Then you've seen the same thing I have. The headings are in the right order — it's the intro that's confusing.

> **Friend:** Hmm. Maybe. What would you change?

> **You:** Just the first two paragraphs. Everything after that works.

> **Friend:** That's a much smaller job than I expected.

> **You:** Yes. And it gets finished.

> **Friend:** You've been very sure about this. Are you sure enough?

> **You:** I might be wrong, but yes. I'd rather rewrite two paragraphs than three.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **the structure's fine, the copy's the problem** — **contrasting with a comma**. Puts the two halves side by side and makes the disagreement structural rather than emotional
- **then you've seen the same thing I have** — a gentle way of saying *you're wrong*, using **"seen"** rather than *"looked"*. **"Look"** implies you may have missed it; **"seen"** implies you didn't
- **maybe** — the single most useful word for making room to change your mind later, even when you're sure
- **that's a much smaller job than I expected** — conceding something true about the other person's plan while still disagreeing
- **I might be wrong, but** — the sentence that costs nothing when you're right, and is the only thing between confidence and arrogance

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *[X]'s fine. [Y]'s the problem. — What would you change? — Just ___. Everything after that works.*

⚠️ **How to be sure without being rude:**
| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| *You're wrong* | *Then you've seen the same thing I have* |
| *That's not right* | *The structure's fine. The copy's the problem* |
| *No, that's wrong* | *Maybe. What would you change?* |

The pattern: **be certain, but hand the question back.** *"Maybe — what would you change?"* sounds like you want to be convinced, which makes disagreement sound open rather than stubborn.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can disagree confidently without an apology
- …you can say *"maybe"* and still be clear you hold your view
- …you can concede something true about the other person's plan 🎯
