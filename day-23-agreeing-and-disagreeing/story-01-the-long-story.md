# Day 23 · 📖 The Long Story — The Time I Was Wrong, Loudly

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I once argued in a meeting for about four minutes about something I was
completely wrong about.** Not uncertain. Not "I might have missed something."
Wrong, confidently, in front of five people, for four minutes. I have thought
about it more than almost any other work thing that's ever happened to me.

What I argued for was that we wouldn't need a database change for a feature we
were about to ship. I had looked at the schema the night before, I was sure, and
my reasoning was sound — the data we needed already existed. That part was
right. What I had missed was a constraint three tables away that nobody had
mentioned in the conversation, and it meant the value we'd have read would have
been the wrong one.

The worst part was not being wrong. It was that I could tell I was enjoying it.
I was doing the thing where you answer faster than the question, where you build
the argument as you go, and where the other person goes quiet because you've
already taken the room. I've watched myself do that to other people and it never
looks good from the outside.

**The person who stopped me was junior to me and had been in the job for four
months.** She said, "I might be wrong, but has anyone checked the status column?"
That was it. One sentence. She was apologising for disagreeing with me, and she
was still the one who was right.

I checked it, she was right, and the feature shipped two weeks later with the
change in it. Nobody said anything else about it, which was somehow worse, because
it meant everyone had noticed and nobody was going to raise it.

What I have taken from it is a rule I now use. If I find myself enjoying an
argument, I treat that as information rather than as evidence — the same way I'd
treat a test passing the first time. So now I say something I've become slightly
embarrassed by, and I say it a lot: *"I might be wrong, but…"* It costs nothing
when you're right, and it is the only thing standing between me and being the
person in that meeting again.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I once ___ for about four minutes about something I was completely wrong about. What I argued for was that ___. What I have taken from it is a rule I now use. — If I ___, I treat that as ___.*

Past perfect for the state before the story (*"I had looked at the schema"*), then simple past for the events. And the last sentence is the shape to steal: **"If I find myself doing X, I treat that as information rather than as evidence."**
