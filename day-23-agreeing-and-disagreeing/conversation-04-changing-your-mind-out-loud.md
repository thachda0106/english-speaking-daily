# Day 23 · Conversation 4 — Changing Your Mind Out Loud

## 🎬 Situation
You argued for something and now you don't believe it. Reversing yourself well is one of the most senior things a person can do.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** You made your case pretty well last week, by the way.

> **You:** Thanks. Though I've been thinking about it since, and I've changed my mind a bit.

> **Friend:** Really? What changed?

> **You:** I looked at what we'd actually have to maintain. It's three services, not one.

> **Friend:** So you're saying we do the simple version first.

> **You:** Yeah. Simple now, and we'll know more about what we need.

> **Friend:** I don't know if that's the right call.

> **You:** Nor am I, entirely. But I'm less sure than I was.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I've changed my mind a bit** — **"a bit"** makes a reversal sound measured rather than fickle. Very useful
- **I looked at what we'd actually have to maintain** — **"actually"** plus the reason you changed. This is the whole sentence
- **nor am I, entirely** — formal but exact: *I'm not entirely sure either*. **"Nor"** without **"neither"** works in a reply
- **I'm less sure than I was** — the most professional way to walk something back. It's about confidence, not preference

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I've changed my mind a bit. — Because I looked at ___. — So ___. — Nor am I entirely, but I'm less sure than I was.*

⚠️ **Reversing well takes three beats:** *name it* (I've changed my mind), *give the new information* (I looked at...), *keep it measured* (a bit / less sure). People forgive the change easily. What they don't forgive is the impression that you were arguing for the argument rather than for the answer.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can reverse a position without sounding inconsistent
- …you can give the new information that changed your mind
- …you can say *"I'm less sure than I was"* instead of *"you're right"* 🎯
