# Day 15 · Conversation 2 — Saying No, Kindly

## 🎬 Situation
You can't go — money, tiredness, a real reason. Saying no is a skill, and "no" is never the hard part. It's what comes after it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** Saturday still on?

> **You:** Ah — actually, I can't this weekend.

> **Friend:** Oh no. Everything alright?

> **You:** Yeah, it's just money this month. I need to sit down and work out some numbers.

> **Friend:** No, that's fine. Are you sure you don't want to come for a bit anyway?

> **You:** I appreciate that, but honestly I'd just be tired company.

> **Friend:** Fair enough. Next weekend though?

> **You:** Definitely. Let me sort this out and I'll text you.

> **Friend:** Take your time. I'll hold you to it.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **Ah — actually, I can't** — the softest possible no. **"Actually"** buys you a moment; **"ah"** softens what follows
- **Everything alright?** — the automatic response to a no in English. Ask it before you explain
- **I'd just be tired company** — saying you'd rather not be around. Genuinely honest and much better than a vague *"maybe not"*
- **Next weekend though?** — keeping the door open. Turning a no into a later yes

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Ah — actually, I can't this weekend. — [short reason]. — I'd just be tired company. — Next weekend though?*

**A good "no" has three parts:** the no, a real reason, and a next time. Without the third part, the invitation is just closed. With it, the other person doesn't feel rejected — just rescheduled.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can say no without inventing an excuse
- …you can follow a no with *"next weekend though?"*
- …you can tell the truth with *"I'd just be tired company"* 🎯
