# Day 15 · Conversation 4 — Your Plan Is Better Than Their Idea

## 🎬 Situation
Someone suggests something and you'd rather do what you had in mind. You can agree without pretending to be enthusiastic.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** I was thinking we could do that climbing place. The new one.

> **You:** Oh — the one near the station?

> **Friend:** Yeah. You free Saturday?

> **You:** I might be, actually. I was also thinking about the market though.

> **Friend:** The market's not really a plan, is it? It's just walking.

> **You:** Fair. Climbing's better — when were you thinking? Afternoon?

> **Friend:** Yeah, about two?

> **You:** Two's good. Do I need to book anything?

> **Friend:** I'll check tonight.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I might be, actually** — agreeing while leaving yourself room. The **"actually"** makes it feel like you just decided
- **I was also thinking about** — putting your own idea forward without contradicting anyone
- **Fair** — conceding a point. One word, and it buys you the next turn
- **when were you thinking?** — steering toward specifics once you've decided yes

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I might be, actually. I was also thinking about ___. — Fair. — When were you thinking?*

**The key move: don't argue for your own plan.** You can win it without ever saying *"no"* — raise it, concede the obvious part (*"it's just walking"*), then ask a logistics question. **The other person usually agrees on their own.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can raise your own option without contradicting anyone
- …you can concede with *"fair"* and still get what you wanted
- …you can agree and then steer to a time 🎯
