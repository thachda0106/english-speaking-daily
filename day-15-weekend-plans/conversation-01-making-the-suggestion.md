# Day 15 · Conversation 1 — Making The Suggestion

## 🎬 Situation
It's Friday and someone says "so, what are we doing this weekend?" The mistake is answering with a single word. English invitations need a shape.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** So — weekend plans, or are we just going to pretend we have plans?

> **You:** I was thinking maybe something low-key. I haven't done anything for two weeks.

> **Friend:** Same. What did you have in mind?

> **You:** There's a market near me on Saturday. Nothing special, just food and things.

> **Friend:** I'm in. What time?

> **You:** Eleven-ish? Or later if you want a lie-in.

> **Friend:** Eleven's good. Where do I meet you?

> **You:** I'll send you the pin — it's about ten minutes' walk from the station.

> **Friend:** Perfect.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I was thinking maybe** — the opening move of every English invitation. It sounds like an idea, not a decision
- **low-key** — *"I'm not doing anything, but I'm not doing nothing"*. Useful and increasingly normal at work too
- **What did you have in mind?** — asking someone to elaborate. Very useful; most questions get one word back
- **I'll send you the pin** — **"pin"** is the standard word for a location on a map. *"Location"* is not

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I was thinking maybe ___. What did you have in mind? — [time]? Or later if you want a lie-in.*

**"I was thinking maybe"** does all the work. It signals *I'm suggesting, not deciding* — which gives the other person room to agree **and** room to counter, so nobody is cornered.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can propose something without sounding like you're issuing an instruction
- …you can ask *"what did you have in mind?"* and get a real answer
- …you can suggest a time and soften it in the same breath 🎯
