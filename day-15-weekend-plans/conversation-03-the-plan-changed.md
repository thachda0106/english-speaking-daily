# Day 15 · Conversation 3 — The Plan Changed

## 🎬 Situation
Plans get made and plans get cancelled. English has a surprisingly rich vocabulary for this, and most people choose the bluntest option available.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** Right, so I'm at the market in like twenty minutes.

> **You:** Ugh — can we push it back? I still haven't finished this thing.

> **Friend:** Sure, how much longer?

> **You:** Maybe an hour? I'm nearly there.

> **Friend:** Fine by me. I'll get a coffee.

> **You:** Sorry, genuinely — I'll make it up to you.

> **Friend:** You don't have to keep apologising.

> **You:** Force of habit.

> **Friend:** Yeah, I noticed. Half eleven then?

> **You:** Half eleven. I'll text you when I'm leaving.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **can we push it back?** — the standard way to delay something. **"push it back"** is the phrase; *"postpone it"* is a formal email word
- **how much longer?** — the question you'll always be asked, so have an answer ready
- **Fine by me** — agreement, slightly more relaxed than *"that works"*
- **you don't have to keep apologising** — telling someone they're over-apologising, which is itself a form of closeness

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Can we push it back? — Maybe an hour? I'm nearly there. — Sorry, genuinely — I'll make it up to you. — Half eleven then?*

⚠️ **The trap:** apologising more than once. In English, second apologies get *more* awkward, not less — the other person has to reassure you. **Apologise once, then switch to a solution.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can delay with *"can we push it back?"*
- …you can give a specific time instead of a vague one
- …you can apologise once and then stop 🎯
