# Day 26 · Conversation 4 — Telling Someone Something Uncomfortable

## 🎬 Situation
You have to deliver bad news. English has an order for this that softens the blow without hiding the point.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** Did it go through in the end?

> **You:** Partly. And I want to be straight with you — the second half failed.

> **Friend:** Okay. How bad is that?

> **You:** Nothing's lost. It's just delayed. Nothing failed permanently.

> **Friend:** That's a relief, actually.

> **You:** I know it's the second time this month. I'm sorry about that.

> **Friend:** It happens. What happens now?

> **You:** We retry it tonight, and this time with the lock. So it can't overlap.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I want to be straight with you** — a formal version of *"I'm going to be honest"*. Excellent for delivering bad news
- **nothing's lost, it's just delayed** — the most reassuring sentence in English, and it costs nothing to say because it's usually partly true
- **it's the second time this month** — owning the pattern, not the incident. Saying *"it happened again"* sounds like you're blaming something else
- **so it can't overlap** — **"so it can't"** for a reason that closes a risk. Very precise engineering English

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I want to be straight with you — [the news]. — Nothing's lost, it's just delayed. — It happened again; I'm sorry about that. — What happens now is ___.*

⚠️ **The order that makes bad news land well:** *news → impact → apology → next step.* Almost every competent person does this without noticing. The order that doesn't is *apology → news*, because you make the other person manage your feelings before they've processed the information.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can deliver bad news in four beats
- …you can separate *"nothing's lost"* from *"it happened again"*
- …you can end a bad-news message with the next step 🎯
