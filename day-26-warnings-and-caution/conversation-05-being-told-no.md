# Day 26 · Conversation 5 — Being Told No

## 🎬 Situation
You've asked for something and been refused. Accepting it well is a skill, and English has a vocabulary for it that sounds like strength rather than defeat.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Manager:** I don't think we can justify it this quarter.

> **You:** Okay. Can I ask what would change that?

> **Manager:** If we hit the number, we look again in January.

> **You:** That works. So it's a timing thing, not a no.

> **Manager:** Not a no. A not-now.

> **You:** I can work with that. What do you need from me to hit the number?

> **Manager:** Just the migration. If that lands clean, we're in a better position.

> **You:** Then let's do that.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I don't think we can justify it** — the polite refusal. **"Justify"** means *give a reason for spending the money*. Very useful
- **what would change that?** — the single best question after a refusal. It turns *"no"* into *"not yet"*
- **it's a timing thing, not a no** — doing the reinterpretation out loud. Risky if you're wrong, which is why you ask first
- **I can work with that** — **"work with"** is better than *"live with"* or *"accept"*. It sounds active, not defeated

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Can I ask what would change that? — So it's ___, not a no. — I can work with that. What do you need from me?*

⚠️ **"What would change that?"** is worth memorising. It does three things at once: it accepts the refusal, it asks for the actual constraint, and it signals you weren't just hoping to be told yes. **Asking it makes you look like someone who wants to solve the problem rather than win the argument.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask *"what would change that?"* without being difficult
- …you can separate *"not now"* from *"no"*
- …you can accept a refusal and redirect to what you can do 🎯
