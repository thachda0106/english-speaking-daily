# Day 26 · Conversation 1 — Warning Someone About A Risk

## 🎬 Situation
Someone is about to do something you think is a mistake. English has a register for this that is direct without being an accusation.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** I'm going to deploy it at five, before everyone leaves.

> **You:** I'd be careful with that.

> **Friend:** Why? It'll be fine.

> **You:** It'll probably be fine. But if it does break at five, there's nobody around who can fix it.

> **Friend:** I can fix it from home.

> **You:** How long does that actually take you?

> **Friend:** …About twenty minutes.

> **You:** Right. So maybe ten, not five. Just because it's more expensive to do now.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **I'd be careful with that** — a soft warning. **"I'd be careful"** is advice, not criticism, which is why it works
- **it'll be fine** — the sentence you need to push back on. It's what you say when you haven't checked
- **if it does break at five** — **"if it does"** is conditional *and* concessive. Very soft way to raise a bad outcome
- **so maybe ten, not five** — **"so maybe X, not Y"** — the pattern that gets a decision changed without an argument

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I'd be careful with that. — It might be fine, but if it does ___, there's nobody who can ___. — So maybe ___, not ___.*

⚠️ **"I'd be careful with that"** is the whole skill. Compare:
- *"That's risky"* — a judgment
- *"You shouldn't do that"* — an order
- *"I'd be careful with that"* — a flag you can both look at

It invites the other person to explain their reasoning, which is how you find out whether they've already considered it.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can raise a risk without saying *"you shouldn't"*
- …you can use *"if it does break…"* to voice a bad outcome softly
- …you can redirect with *"so maybe X, not Y"* 🎯
