# Day 13 · Conversation 3 — On The Bus With Someone

## 🎬 Situation
You're sitting next to someone. Neither of you knows the other. This is the closest thing English has to a ritual stranger conversation, and it has rules.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Stranger:** Mind if I sit here?

> **You:** No, go ahead.

> **Stranger:** Thanks. Are you getting off at the next stop?

> **You:** No, I'm going right to the end.

> **Stranger:** Lucky you. It's been rammed all morning.

> **You:** Yeah, I nearly didn't get on. I was waiting for two buses to go past.

> **Stranger:** They come every ten minutes, don't they?

> **You:** Which is fine until it's four in the afternoon.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **Mind if I sit here?** — the standard question. **"Mind"** is British for *"do you mind"*; American English says *"Do you mind…"*
- **no, go ahead** — the complete answer. Not *"you can"*, not *"it's fine"*
- **rammed** — very British for *"completely full"*. Informal and vivid
- **which is fine until it's four in the afternoon** — agreeing, then adding the part that matters

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Mind if I sit here? — No, go ahead. — Are you getting off here? — Which is fine until ___.

**The rule of bus small talk:** ask about the *journey*, never about the *person*. Stops, timing, how busy it is — all safe. Age, salary, relationship status — no.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can open a conversation with a stranger safely
- …you can say *"no, go ahead"* as one automatic phrase
- …you can keep it going with a *"which is fine until…"* turn 🎯
