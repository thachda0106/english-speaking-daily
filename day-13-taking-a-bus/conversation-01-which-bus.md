# Day 13 · Conversation 1 — Which Bus?

## 🎬 Situation
You're at a stop with a bus in five minutes and you don't know if it's the right one. Asking clearly is the whole skill here.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me, does this bus go to the city centre?

> **Driver:** It does. It stops at the station and then the market.

> **You:** Great. How many stops is that, roughly?

> **Driver:** About twelve. Maybe fifteen minutes if the traffic's bad.

> **You:** And do I need a ticket beforehand?

> **Driver:** You can buy one on the bus, from me.

> **You:** Perfect, thank you.

> **Driver:** Just get on and I'll sort you out.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **does this bus go to ___?** — the question that matters. *"Which bus goes to…?"* asks about numbers; *"does this bus go to…?"* asks about **this one**
- **roughly** — asking for an approximation without needing precision. Sounds natural rather than demanding
- **if the traffic's bad** — a condition. **"if the traffic's"** is the short form of *"if the traffic is"*
- **I'll sort you out** — British and informal for *"I'll handle it"*

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Excuse me, does this bus go to ___? — It does. — How many stops, roughly? — Do I need a ticket beforehand?*

Four short questions, one bus ride. This is the practical core of the whole day — get it automatic and getting around becomes unstressful.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can confirm a bus goes where you want without guessing
- …you can ask about time using *"roughly"* or *"about how long"*
- …you can buy a ticket on board without apologising 🎯
