# Day 13 · Conversation 4 — Reading The Display

## 🎬 Situation
The stop is coming up. The display says something you don't quite understand — a place name, a "via", or a number. This is a practical skill, not a small talk one.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Excuse me, does this one go to the hospital or the university?

> **Driver:** Hospital. The university one's the 47.

> **You:** Oh — I need the 47 then. Is this stop no good?

> **Driver:** It's fine, you can get the 47 at the next stop. It goes right past.

> **You:** And how long will that take?

> **Driver:** Five minutes, maybe eight.

> **You:** And then I'm there?

> **Driver:** Then you're there. Look for the big sign — you can't miss it.

> **You:** Perfect, thank you so much.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **does this one go to X or Y?** — asking to choose between two destinations in one question
- **is this stop no good?** — asking whether a stop is usable. Slightly informal; **"is this stop okay?"** is the neutral version
- **then I'm there?** — checking arrival. Two words. You will use it constantly
- **you can't miss it** — reassurance. **"Can't miss it"** is a fixed phrase

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *does this one go to X or Y? — X. — Is this stop no good? — Then I'm there?*

**"Then I'm there?"** is worth learning as one fixed phrase. It's the cheapest way to confirm you've understood a route, and it makes you sound like you've done this before.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can choose between two destinations in one question
- …you can ask whether a stop is usable
- …you can confirm arrival with *"then I'm there?"* 🎯
