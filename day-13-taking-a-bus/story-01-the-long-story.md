# Day 13 · 📖 The Long Story — The Bus I Got On By Mistake

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadowing the
> sentences you like. Then answer the speaking challenge below without stopping.

**I have never been good with buses.** Not in my home city, and certainly not
here. My first week in this country I got on the wrong one twice in one day, and
the second time it took me forty minutes to notice.

The first time was honest ignorance. I asked the driver whether the bus went to
the station, and he said it did, and I got on with a fair amount of confidence,
and then we drove past a warehouse district I had never heard of and kept going.
I only worked it out when I saw a sign for a place I wasn't looking for.

The second time was worse, because I knew the route by then and still got it
wrong. I'd had a long day and I got on without checking the number, and the
numbers are laid out so that 14 and 41 look identical from the side of the road.
I got on the 41, which goes out to an industrial estate, and it took me about
twenty minutes to admit to myself that I was on the wrong bus.

What I did next is the part I actually remember, because it was the first time I
used English to fix a problem rather than just survive one. I got off at the next
stop, went back to where the bus had been, and asked the driver a question I had
never asked before: "does this one go to the station, or is that a different
number?" He told me that 14 was the one I wanted, that it came back in eight
minutes, and that I should get off at the next stop rather than the one I was
standing at. Then he said, and I remember this clearly, "no worries."

Three words. That was the entire conversation, and it worked. I have thought
about "no worries" more than I have thought about most grammar, because it
does something English rarely does: it closes the mistake without opening the
door to it.

What I learned is that the fear isn't the wrong bus. The fear is being stuck in
it alone. The bus is a public space full of people who have all been lost on it,
and every one of them will answer your question if you ask it. The only thing
that has to happen first is that you say the question out loud.

I still check the number every single time. But I no longer feel the thing I
used to feel at the stop, which is that everyone can see I don't know what I'm
doing. Nobody can see that. I have checked.

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *I have never been good at ___. The first time was ___. The second time was worse, because ___. What I did next is the part I remember, because ___.

Present perfect for a life fact — *"I have never been good at buses"* — then past simple for the story. That contrast is what makes the opening land.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **has the ___ just gone?** · **then I'm there?** · **is this stop no good?** · **of course** · **I'll just walk, then** · **no worries** · **mind if I sit here?** · **it can't miss it** → *"you can't miss it"*
