# Day 13 · Conversation 5 — Missing It, And Walking Home

## 🎬 Situation
The bus has gone. You were supposed to get off three stops ago, or you missed it because you were looking at your phone. This happens. The vocabulary here is about being unhurried about it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **You:** Has the 14 just gone?

> **Other:** Yeah, about a minute ago.

> **You:** Of course. How long till the next one?

> **Other:** About ten, I think. Or twelve — it depends.

> **You:** No worries. Do you know if there's a bus from here that goes into town?

> **Other:** There's the 22, but I think that goes the other way.

> **You:** Right. I'll just walk, then. It's about twenty minutes.

> **Other:** It's not too bad that way. There's a park most of the way.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **has the 14 just gone?** — **"just gone"** is the natural past perfect. *"Has just left"* is American
- **of course** — the flat, slightly rueful reaction when something obvious happens to you. Not an apology
- **no worries** — Australian and British English for *"that's fine"*, and it works anywhere
- **I'll just walk, then** — **"just"** here means "simply, without making a fuss". **"Then"** at the end closes the decision

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Has the ___ just gone? — Yeah, about a minute ago. — How long till the next one? — I'll just walk, then.*

**"Of course"** and **"I'll just…"** are the two halves of not beating yourself up. Say them out loud until they feel normal.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can ask whether a bus has just left without sounding lost
- …you can say *"of course"* and *"I'll just…"* without apologising
- …you can get information from another passenger without a long introduction 🎯
