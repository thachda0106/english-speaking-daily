# Day 30 · 📖 The Long Story — The Thirty Days I Kept Talking

> The last day of the course. This story is not about anything that happened — it is about the one thing that actually changed. Read it out loud twice, shadow the audio, then retell it in 60 seconds. You have earned it.

---

**I started this thirty days ago because I wanted to speak English like a person, and today I understood that I already do.**

On the first day I sat in my kitchen and tried to say one sentence out loud to nobody. "I went to the market yesterday." I said it four times. It felt strange, because I had said that sentence a thousand times in my head, and never out loud with nobody there. That was the first thing that was hard, and the easiest thing I have ever started.

The early days were slow. I learned to order coffee, and I could not remember the word for *large*, so I said "the big one" and pointed. I learned to ask for directions and I still pointed at the map. I learned to buy things and I counted the money on my fingers because my brain was translating everything from Vietnamese first. Nothing worked well, and I kept saying the words anyway, because there was no other way to learn them.

Around the second week something small changed. I was at a shop and I asked for something without thinking about the English first. Just "How much is this?" and the answer came back and I understood it. I did not translate anything. That was the first time, and I stood there for a second because I had not noticed it happening.

The third week I did the thing I was most afraid of: I told a story. I told the tea story — how a man gave me tea when I ordered coffee — to a colleague at work. I said, "Actually, something funny happened," and then four sentences, and he laughed. My grammar was not good. There was one word in the wrong place the whole way through. He laughed anyway, and then he told me about his cooking. That was the moment I understood that nobody was listening to my grammar. They were listening to my story.

The fourth week I lost words. I still do. What changed is that I stopped treating it as a problem. I say "you know what I mean" and keep going. I say "sorry, let me start again," and start again. I do not apologise five times. I lost a word last Tuesday in the middle of a meeting and said "I'm looking for it," found it, and continued, and nobody noticed at all.

So today, thirty days later, what changed? My vocabulary did not get much bigger. I still cannot find some words, and my grammar is still simple. But I stopped waiting to be good enough to talk. That was the whole thing. I was waiting for permission that was never coming, and every day I did not speak I lost a little more of the habit.

**Here is what I know now that I did not know thirty days ago. Fluency is not speaking without mistakes. It is speaking while making them.** A person who pauses, searches, says "I mean," and finishes the sentence is far easier to talk to than a person who says nothing at all. Thirty days ago I could not do that. Today I do it without noticing, and that is not a small thing — that is the whole thing.

## 🧠 Story vocabulary & grammar
- **Past-tense verbs you just read:** `started · wanted · said · went · said · felt · had said · was · said · did · said · pointed · learned · could not remember · said · asked · still pointed · bought · counted · using · was · nothing worked · kept saying · happened · was · asked · understood · translated · stood · noticed · told · happened · said · wrong · laughed · started · lost · do · said · treating · said · lost · found · continued · changed · got · waiting · doing · lost
- Notice the **present perfect appears near the end**: *"What changed is my vocabulary did not get much bigger"* — actually present simple. But *"I stopped treating it as a problem"* and *"He stopped treating it"* — the whole story is past simple, and that is correct for a finished thirty-day period. **Finished time → past simple.** You already know this from Day 5; now it is automatic.
- 🎯 **The grammar of the last paragraph is the grammar of the course:** *"Fluency is not speaking without mistakes. It is speaking while making them."* Two parallel sentences, the second negating the first. This *not X, but Y* pattern is the most persuasive shape in English — and it is what you have been practising in every conversation since Day 5.
