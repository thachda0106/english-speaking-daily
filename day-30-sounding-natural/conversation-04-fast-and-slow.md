# Day 30 · Conversation 4 — Talking Fast, and Slowing Down

## 🎬 Situation
Two opposite problems, one solution: know when to speed up and when to slow down. Fluency is rhythm, not speed.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** You speak really fast when you're excited.

> **You:** I do? Sorry, I didn't know. I can slow down.

> **Friend:** No, it's not bad. It just means I can't hear everything.

> **You:** That makes sense. So I should speak slowly when it matters.

> **Friend:** Yeah, exactly. Fast is fine for stories. Slow is fine for numbers.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`speak · fast · excited · don't know · slow down · not bad · hear everything · makes sense · should · speak slowly · matters · yeah · exactly · fine · stories · numbers`

## ✅ After you speak
- The rhythm formula = **notice it + adjust it + state the rule**:
  *"That makes sense. So I should speak slowly when it matters."*
- 💡 **"I do?"** — the short surprise reply. Two words, very natural, and it keeps a conversation alive without needing any vocabulary.
- 💡 **"That makes sense."** — 🎯 **your best agreement.** It says *I understand and it is logical* — stronger than *"okay"*, and it works in interviews, meetings, and small talk. You used it on Day 5; now it should be automatic.
- 💡 **"That makes sense"** vs **"That sounds good"** — *makes sense* = the logic fits. *sounds good* = I like it. Using the wrong one is a small error that changes your meaning. Choose deliberately.
- 💡 **"Fast is fine for stories. Slow is fine for numbers."** — 🎯 a **pattern sentence**: *X is fine for A. Y is fine for B.* Two short halves, easy to say, and it sounds composed and adult. Learn this pattern and use it whenever you want to sound confident.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-04` and answer. **Today: say "That makes sense" out loud.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** do **not** aim to speak slowly always. Slow English with bad rhythm sounds careful; fast English with good rhythm sounds fluent. **The variable is the pause, not the speed.** People understand fast speech perfectly — they stop understanding when the pauses are in the wrong place. Practise the *pause before the important word*, not the whole sentence.
