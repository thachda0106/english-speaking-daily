# Day 30 · Conversation 3 — The Fillers That Buy You Time

## 🎬 Situation
You need two seconds to think. Without a filler, the gap sounds like the end of your sentence. With one, the gap sounds like you are thinking.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** What do you want to do this weekend?

> **You:** Hmm. I mean, I was thinking maybe visiting a friend. Actually, I don't know yet.

> **Friend:** That's fine. You could just rest.

> **You:** Yeah, well, I probably will. I need to do the shopping first anyway.

> **Friend:** Good plan. Let me know what you decide.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`want · do · weekend · thinking · maybe · visiting · friend · actually · don't know yet · fine · rest · probably · shopping · first · anyway · good plan · decide · let me know`

## ✅ After you speak
- The thinking-sound formula = **filler + a short answer + a filler + a real answer**:
  *"Hmm. I mean, I was thinking maybe visiting a friend. Actually, I don't know yet."*
- 💡 **"I mean…"** — 🎯 **the universal filler.** Works before *any* word. It does not mean anything; it buys you a second and signals thought. Native speakers use it constantly and most learners never use it at all.
- 💡 **"Well…"** — what you say when the other person's last sentence changed the question. It is a small reset button. *"Yeah, well, I probably will."*
- 💡 **"Actually…"** — gently corrects or changes what you were about to say. **"Actually, I don't know yet."** sounds honest rather than unprepared.
- 💡 **"Let me know what you decide."** — the warm, very natural way to end an open decision. Better than a formal *"Please tell me."*
- 🎙️ Play `friend-03` and answer. **Today: say "I mean…" and "Well…" out loud five times each.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** **fillers are not mistakes — they are punctuation.** Writing has commas and "well". Speech has *"I mean"*, *"actually"*, *"you know"*. A learner who avoids fillers sounds robotic; a learner who uses them sounds like a person thinking. The goal is not to remove your pauses. It is to **cover them with a sound**, so the other person hears a speaker, not a search.
