# Day 30 · Conversation 2 — Starting Over Without Dying Inside

## 🎬 Situation
You realise halfway through a sentence that it has gone wrong. The mistake is not the problem. The panic is the problem.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** Tell me about your weekend.

> **You:** I go to the — sorry, let me start again. I went to a market near my house.

> **Friend:** Okay. What did you buy?

> **You:** Vegetables and fish. And then I cooked it at home. It was okay.

> **Friend:** What was wrong with it?

> **You:** Nothing was wrong. It just wasn't very good.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`tell me · weekend · sorry · let me start again · went · market · near · house · okay · buy · vegetables · fish · cooked · home · wrong · wasn't · good · very good · just wasn't`

## ✅ After you speak
- The restart formula = **name it + restart from a clean sentence**:
  *"Sorry, let me start again. I went to a market near my house."*
- 💡 **"Sorry, let me start again."** — 🎯 **the restart sentence.** Five words, and they buy you a completely fresh beginning. Most learners are afraid of restarting because it feels like admitting defeat. It is the opposite — it is the most confident thing a speaker can do.
- 💡 **"I go to the — sorry."** — even more useful: restart **mid-sentence** without apologising twice. Catch it early and no one even noticed. The pause is the whole event, and it is invisible if you move on quickly.
- 💡 **"It just wasn't very good."** — the *just* + *very* pattern. It makes a criticism gentle without making it vague. *"It was bad"* is too strong. *"It just wasn't very good"* is honest and kind.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-02` and answer. **Today: restart one sentence out loud with "let me start again."**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** every fluent speaker's inner voice says *"that's wrong."* The difference is what happens next. A learner stops and apologises twice; a fluent speaker says *"let me start again"* and goes. **Restarting is a skill, not a symptom.** Practise it until it feels like nothing.
