# Day 16 · Conversation 4 — Talking About The Heat

## 🎬 Situation
It's too hot to be outside. This is a real conversation with a real vocabulary, and "it's too hot to ___" is the sentence that opens it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** It's too hot to be outside right now.

> **You:** It's ridiculous. I walked to the shop and it took about ten minutes to feel stupid.

> **Friend:** I stayed in. I was going to go to the park and then just didn't.

> **You:** Smart. It's the kind of heat that makes you feel guilty for being indoors.

> **Friend:** Exactly. The flat feels wrong in this weather.

> **You:** It's too hot to sleep and too cold to have the windows open. Perfect.

> **Friend:** I keep forgetting and then remembering in the same hour.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **It's too hot to ___** — **"too…to"** takes a base verb: *too hot to sleep*, *too cold to open*, *too late to bother*. Never *"too hot sleeping"*
- **took about ten minutes to feel stupid** — a small funny complaint. **"feel stupid"** is more natural than *"feel bad"*
- **made you feel guilty for being indoors** — **make somebody feel + adjective**. Very common
- **forgetting and then remembering in the same hour** — the wrongness of heat, said properly

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *It's too hot to ___. — I [did the thing] and it took ___ to feel ___. — The kind of heat that makes you feel ___.*

⚠️ **"Too" always needs "to."** *It's too hot to be out*, *I'm too tired to go*, *it's too expensive to buy*. And after *"too…to"*, use the **base verb**: *"too hot to sleep"*, never *"too hot sleeping"*.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can use *"too + adjective + to + verb"* without dropping anything
- …you can say *"the kind of weather that makes you…"*
- …you can describe a bad day with no complaint about other people 🎯
