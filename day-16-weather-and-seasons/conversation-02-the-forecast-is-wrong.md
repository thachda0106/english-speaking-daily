# Day 16 · Conversation 2 — The Forecast Is Wrong

## 🎬 Situation
The weather app said one thing and the sky did another. Complaining about this is a global sport, and English has very specific vocabulary for it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** The forecast said sunny all day. What is that?

> **You:** I gave up on the forecast about a year ago.

> **Friend:** It said fourteen degrees. It's about nine.

> **You:** Fourteen is a windchill, not a temperature.

> **Friend:** That's it exactly.

> **You:** And even when it's right about the temperature, it's never right about when.

> **Friend:** Do you even check it now?

> **You:** Every morning. Out of habit. It changes nothing about what I wear.

> **Friend:** I check it about nine times a day. Which is never helpful.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **What is that?** — the natural reaction to weather that has ignored the forecast. Genuinely ambiguous, which is why it works
- **fourteen degrees** — in British English **"degrees"** is understood without saying Celsius. Safer than guessing wrong
- **the forecast said ___ / it's about ___** — the past-tense pattern for complaining about a prediction
- **It changes nothing about what I wear** — admitting the forecast is useless, which is funny and true

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *The forecast said ___ and it's ___. — I gave up on the forecast ___. — It changes nothing about what I ___.*

**"The forecast said X and it's Y"** is a complete, satisfying sentence that requires no further content. It's the easiest thing in English to say when you have nothing interesting to contribute.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can complain about the forecast using past tense
- …you can say *"it changes nothing"* about something you keep doing anyway
- …you can fill a silence with weather without it feeling like filler 🎯
