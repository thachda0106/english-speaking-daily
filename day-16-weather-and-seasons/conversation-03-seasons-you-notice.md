# Day 16 · Conversation 3 — Seasons You Actually Notice

## 🎬 Situation
Somebody asks what season you prefer. This is easier than it sounds, because almost everyone has a genuine answer — they just rarely say it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** What's your favourite season?

> **You:** Autumn, definitely.

> **Friend:** Really? I thought you'd say summer.

> **You:** No — summer here is just heat with no break in it.

> **Friend:** What's autumn got going for it?

> **You:** The light. It comes in lower, everything looks better for about four weeks, and then it's gone.

> **Friend:** That's annoyingly specific.

> **You:** It's the one thing I'd miss if I moved somewhere else.

> **Friend:** I'd miss spring. Everything's actually growing, so it feels earned somehow.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **What's your favourite season?** — asked in almost every English conversation at least once
- **autumn** / **fall** — British / American. Both are understood everywhere, so don't worry about which to use
- **What's ___ got going for it?** — asking someone to justify a preference. Informal, and it invites a real answer
- **somehow** — a small word that adds a feeling you can't quite explain. Native speakers use it constantly

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Autumn, definitely. — [what it does that the other doesn't]. — It's the one thing I'd miss if I ___.*

⚠️ **Never drop the "to".** A very common learner slip is leaving it out:
- ✅ *It's supposed **to** rain this afternoon.*
- ❌ *It's supposed rain this afternoon.*

Same trap with *"should"* and *"might"* — *"it's supposed **to** rain"*, *"it should **be** fine"*, *"it might **snow**"*. Each needs that little word, and omitting it makes the sentence sound like a note torn out of context.

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can answer *"what's your favourite season?"* with a reason
- …you can say *"it's the one thing I'd miss if I…"*
- …you can use *"somehow"* to add a feeling you can't explain 🎯
