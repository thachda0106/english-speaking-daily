# Day 16 · Conversation 5 — The First Cold Day

## 🎬 Situation
The temperature drops and everybody mentions it. This is the moment English people actually enjoy complaining, and it's easier than you'd think.

## 🗣️ Dialogue

> **Friend:** Did you feel it this morning? First proper cold day.

> **You:** Yeah, I walked to the bus and didn't regret it for once.

> **Friend:** I did regret it. Wore a t-shirt, obviously.

> **You:** You always wear a t-shirt.

> **Friend:** It's a medical condition at this point.

> **You:** Is it normally this cold in November?

> **Friend:** No, it's about five degrees colder than usual. The forecast said it wouldn't be like this.

> **You:** Well. It's here now, so.

> **Friend:** Bring a coat tomorrow. I'm not asking.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary

- **Did you feel it this morning?** — *"it"* = the weather change. One word carries the whole meaning
- **didn't regret it for once** — **"for once"** implies a pattern. Very useful for complaining affectionately
- **It's a medical condition at this point** — self-deprecation as humour. Extremely common among friends
- **It's here now, so** — accepting something unpleasant without a full sentence. Deliberately flat, and it lands

## ✅ After you speak

**Formula:** *Did you feel it this morning? — [Did / didn't] regret it. — [weather] it is about ___ colder than usual.*

⚠️ **A very useful habit:** native speakers often end an agreeing turn with something short and flat — *"Well. It's here now, so."* — because a full sentence would be over-committed. **Short agreement is normal English, not laziness.**

## ✅ Done when…
- …you can say *"did you feel it?"* and mean the weather
- …you can use *"for once"* to imply a habit
- …you can agree briefly without sounding like you have nothing to say 🎯
