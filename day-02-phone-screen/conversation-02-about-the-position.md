# Day 2 · Conversation 2 — About the Position

## 🎬 Situation
The recruiter explains the job. Your job here is to ask questions and show that the position matches your skills — this is where you "sell" your experience.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Recruiter:** So, let me tell you about the position. We're looking for a backend developer for our team.

> **You:** That sounds interesting. Could you tell me more about the team and the project?

> **Recruiter:** Of course. The team works on a system for online orders. We have about twenty developers.

> **You:** I see. And what technology does the team use?

> **Recruiter:** We use Node.js and PostgreSQL, and we're moving to a new microservices architecture.

> **You:** That's great — I already work with Node.js and PostgreSQL, so it's a good match.

> **Recruiter:** Excellent! It sounds like you have the experience we need.

> **You:** Thank you. I'm excited about the opportunity.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`position · backend · developer · team · project · system · online · orders · developers · technology · Node.js · PostgreSQL · microservices · architecture · work · good match · experience · excited · opportunity`

## ✅ After you speak
- 💡 **"good match"** — a very natural way to say you fit the job. *"Your skills are a good match for this role."*
- This conversation is your chance to connect THEIR stack to YOUR stack: Node.js → Node.js, PostgreSQL → PostgreSQL. The model answer does it — memorize that pattern: **"I already work with X, so it's a good match."**
- 🎙️ Play `recruiter-02`. For the last answer, use YOUR real stack (NestJS? Prisma?) and your real project type.
