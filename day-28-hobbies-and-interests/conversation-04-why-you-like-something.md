# Day 28 · Conversation 4 — Why You Like Something

## 🎬 Situation
Someone asks *why* you like something. This is where most answers stop working — you either say "it's good" and go quiet, or you over-explain.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** You said you like football. Why do you like it?

> **You:** Because it's the only thing I do with other people. I work alone all day, so the weekend is different.

> **Friend:** That's a good reason. I didn't think about it like that.

> **You:** What about you? What do you like about your hobby?

> **Friend:** Cooking, mostly. Because at the end I have something I made. You can't eat a book.

> **You:** That's a better reason than mine. Mine is just "it's fun."

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`why · like · because · only thing · other people · alone · all day · weekend · different · good reason · think · hobby · cooking · mostly · end · something · made · can't eat · better reason · mine · just fun`

## ✅ After you speak
- The why-answer formula = **because + one true reason, in your own life**:
  *"Because it's the only thing I do with other people. I work alone all day, so the weekend is different."*
- 💡 **"I didn't think about it like that."** — 🎯 **the best reaction you can get.** It tells the person you said something new. You need this sentence as much as you need good opinions.
- 💡 **"Because at the end I have something I made."** — a *reason*, not a quality. *"It's delicious"* is a quality (describes the food). *"At the end I have something I made"* is a reason (explains why you like cooking). Reasons are better in conversation.
- 💡 **"You can't eat a book."** — a joke. Jokes are allowed. You do not need to be clever, you just need to be light.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-04` and answer. **Today: answer one "why do you like it?" with a real reason.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** *"Because"* is the most powerful word you own in English. It connects two sentences into one thought. Every learner knows *because*, and most learners use it only in explanations. Use it for **opinions** too: *"I like it because…"* — that single pattern turns any opinion into a real reason.
