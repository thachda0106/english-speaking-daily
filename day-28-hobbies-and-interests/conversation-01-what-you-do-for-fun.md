# Day 28 · Conversation 1 — What You Do For Fun

## 🎬 Situation
Someone asks what you do outside work. The honest answer is often "not much" — and that is completely fine. This conversation is about saying it without apologising for it.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** So, what do you do when you're not working?

> **You:** Not much, honestly. I watch videos and play football on the weekend.

> **Friend:** Football? Do you play well?

> **You:** I'm okay. I play with friends, and we lose more than we win.

> **Friend:** That's still fun, though. Do you watch football too?

> **You:** Sometimes, but I don't watch it every match. I only watch when my team plays.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`what do you do · working · not much · honestly · watch · videos · play · football · weekend · well · friends · lose · win · still · fun · watch · sometimes · every · match · only · team`

## ✅ After you speak
- The hobbies formula = **answer honestly + name two things + add one detail**:
  *"Not much, honestly. I watch videos and play football on the weekend — with friends. We lose more than we win."*
- 💡 **"Not much, honestly."** — the honest answer that works. No apology, no embarrassment. Just true. 🎯
- 💡 **"I'm okay."** — the natural answer to *"Do you play well?"* Do not say *"Yes, a little"* (too boastful) or *"No"* (too negative). *"I'm okay"* is calm and true.
- 💡 **"We lose more than we win."** — a specific, funny detail. This is what makes people remember you. One real detail beats a list of hobbies every time.
- 💡 **"I only watch when my team plays."** — the *only … when* pattern. It makes an opinion sound precise, not vague.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-01` and answer. **Today: answer "What do you do for fun?" out loud in three sentences.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** hobbies in conversation are not about impressing anyone. They are about giving the other person something to react to. *"I play football"* is a wall. *"I play football with friends and we lose more than we win"* is an open door.
