# Day 28 · Conversation 2 — Learning Something New

## 🎬 Situation
You are learning something and you cannot do it well yet. English has a very useful habit here: you can talk about being bad at something without sounding bad at English.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Friend:** Have you always played football?

> **You:** No, I started about two years ago. I don't know the rules very well.

> **Friend:** Really? I would think you'd know. It looks easy.

> **You:** Playing is easy. The hard part is when everyone runs faster than me.

> **Friend:** That's funny. Are you learning anything else?

> **You:** I'm trying to cook. Last week I made rice and it was too hard.

> **Friend:** Rice should be easy!

> **You:** You would think so. But mine came out harder than a stone.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`always · started · two years ago · don't know · rules · very well · really · think · look · easy · playing · hard · everyone · run · faster · learning · anything else · trying · cook · last week · rice · come out · stone`

## ✅ After you speak
- The new-skill formula = **when you started + what you can't do yet + a small joke**:
  *"I started about two years ago. I don't know the rules very well — playing is easy, but the hard part is when everyone runs faster than me."*
- 💡 **"You would think so."** — 🎯 **one of the best phrases in English for disagreeing cheerfully.** It says *"sure, you'd think that, but here's the thing"* — all in three words. It turns a correction into a joke. Use it when someone doesn't expect your answer.
- 💡 **"I'm trying to cook."** — *trying to* is honest without being negative. It says you are in the middle of learning, not that you failed.
- 💡 **"It looks easy."** — a very natural compliment. Complimenting *how something looks* is easier than complimenting *a person*, and native speakers use it all the time.
- 🎙️ Play `friend-02` and answer. **Today: say "You would think so" out loud.** It works in almost any conversation.
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** learning something in front of other people is risky in any language. English gives you a safety net: *"I'm trying"* + *"You would think so"* + a joke about yourself. Laughing at your own mistake is the fastest way to make other people comfortable. It is not weakness — it is social fluency.
