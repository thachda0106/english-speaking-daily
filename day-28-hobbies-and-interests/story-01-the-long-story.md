# Day 28 · 📖 The Long Story — The Things I Do Alone

> Hobbies sound like a small topic, but this story is about something bigger: what you say about yourself when nobody is asking you to be impressive. Read it out loud twice, shadow the audio, then retell it in 60 seconds.

---

**I have never told anyone at work that I play football, and I decided to change that last Saturday.**

For a long time, when people at work asked what I do after work, I gave a short answer. "Not much." Then I changed the subject, because I thought football was not interesting. I am not a professional. I am not even good. I go to a small field on Sunday with three friends, and we usually lose.

Last Saturday one of my colleagues asked me a different question. He said, "You look tired today. What did you do yesterday?" I said, "I played football." He said, "Really? I didn't know you played." I told him a little about it — Sunday, three friends, a small field near the station — and then I said the part I never say. I said, "It's the only thing I do with other people."

That sentence surprised me as soon as I said it. I work alone all day. My work is code, and code is quiet; you can spend four hours on a problem without speaking to anyone. On Sunday, for two hours, I am not alone. That is why I like it. Before that day I did not know that was the reason. I just knew I liked football.

Then my colleague told me about his hobby. He cooks. He said, "At the end of the day, I have something I made. You can't eat a book." I laughed, because it was a good answer. Mine had been just "it's fun" — and fun is not a reason, it is only a feeling. His was a reason. He gets something at the end. I get people. Same question, completely different answers, and his was better than mine.

After that, on Sunday, I told my friends I was learning to cook. They laughed at me, because I am not good at it. Last week I made rice and it came out harder than a stone. One of my friends said, "You would think rice should be easy." And that was true. I did think that. I was wrong, and it was funny, and nobody was angry.

Now I say it when people ask. "Not much, honestly. I watch videos and play football on the weekend." Then I add one real thing, so the answer is not empty. Sometimes I say, "I lose more than I win." Sometimes I say, "I'm trying to cook, but last week I burned the rice." Those two sentences are worth more than a long list of hobbies, because they are true and they make people ask me a question.

**I learned something small and a little embarrassing: I was hiding things I liked, because I was afraid they were not good enough.** But nobody was going to judge my football. They were going to ask about it, and then they would tell me about cooking. That is how people become friends in any language — not by being interesting, but by being willing to say something true.

## 🧠 Story vocabulary & grammar
- **Past-tense verbs you just read:** `have · told · decided · gave · changed · thought · was · am · go · lose · asked · did · said · told · said · was · spend · have · said · told · make · get · said · laugh · was · learn · make · come · say · say · watch · play · add · say · say · burn · were · happen · become
- Notice **"the only thing I do with other people"** — *the only* plus a simple present. English loves *the only* for reasons: *"the only thing I…"*, *"the only time I…"*, *"the only way I…"*. It makes any answer sound personal and considered.
- 🎯 **"Fun" is a feeling, not a reason.** *"It's fun"* ends a why-question. *"At the end I have something I made"* continues it. This is the difference between describing and explaining.
