# Day 9 · Conversation 2 — Talking About Food

## 🎬 Situation
You are at the table. Everyone is choosing food. This conversation is only about **food** — but food talk is perfect practice because the sentences are short, and you already know every word. The skill is **adding a little extra** so you don't sound like a robot.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Colleague:** What are you going to order?

> **You:** I think I'll have the chicken rice bowl. I heard it was good.

> **Colleague:** Great choice. I always get the grilled pork. It's really good.

> **You:** Is it spicy? I can't eat very spicy food.

> **Colleague:** A little, but not too much. The sauce is on the side, so you can control it.

> **You:** Oh, that's good to know. Then I'll try it next time.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`order · chicken · rice · bowl · heard · choice · grilled · pork · spicy · can't eat · sauce · side · control · next time · try`

## ✅ After you speak
- The food-talk formula = **decide + give a reason + ask about the details**:
  *"I think I'll have the chicken rice bowl. I heard it was good. Is it spicy?"*
- 💡 **"Is it spicy?"** — the magic question. It works with food, and it also works for work: *"Is the meeting strict about time?"* *"Is the API slow for everyone, or just me?"*
- 💡 **"That's good to know."** = a useful reaction that keeps the conversation alive. It means "I didn't know that, and it's useful." Use it every day at work.
- 💡 **"Then I'll try it next time."** — small talk lives in the future. People like talking about what they'll do later. It makes you easy to talk to.
- 🎙️ Play `colleague-02` and answer. **Today: ask one "Is it ___?" question at lunch** (spicy, far, busy, open).
