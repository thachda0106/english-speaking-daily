# Day 9 · 📖 The Long Story — The First Lunch I Was Not Afraid Of

> One long story for the whole day. Read it out loud twice, shadow the audio, then retell it in your own words. It trains the thing small talk really is — **talking about nothing, on purpose, with people you don't know well.**

---

**Last Wednesday was the first time I joined my teammates for lunch, and I was nervous the whole morning.**

I'm still new at this company — about two months now — and English is not easy for me. In Vietnam, work friends eat together, but at work here everyone has their own lunch, and I didn't want to bother anyone. So I usually eat alone at my desk. I told myself I was too busy, but that was not true. I was just afraid.

The morning I worked normally. I finished a small change and sent the pull request. Then at noon, Duy came to my desk and said, "Hey, are you coming to lunch with us?" My first reaction was to say no. I wanted to tell him, "No thanks, I already ate." But I hadn't eaten. I was just scared.

I said, "Sure, I'd love to. What time are you going?" He said twelve thirty, and four of us walked to a small place near the office. I've passed that place every day for two months and never went in.

Duy ordered first. He got a grilled pork bowl, and he told me the sauce comes on the side, so I can control the spice. That's a small thing, but I learned it in two seconds. We talked about the restaurant first, because that's the easiest topic in the world. Then someone asked about the weekend, and Duy said he went hiking with his girlfriend — ten kilometers, and his legs were sore for two days. I laughed and said I would need a week to recover. People laughed too. That was the moment I felt a little bit normal.

Then Ken asked what I do here. This was the question I was afraid of, because I always talk too much about my work. I said, "I work on the backend side — mostly the database and the API." Then I stopped, because I didn't want to be boring. But Ken asked a follow-up question: "Is it interesting so far?" So I said honestly that the problems are real and I like that. Then I said the most honest thing of the day: the code is fine, but explaining my decisions in English is still hard for me. Ken said, "That's normal, my English is not good either." I didn't expect that. It made me feel much better.

We finished lunch at one o'clock. Duy looked at his phone and said, "It's already one o'clock, huh." I laughed — of course he did that — and we went back to the office. I said, "See you this afternoon!"

After that, I thought about the whole lunch. I didn't learn any new words. I didn't use any big English. I just ate, answered, and asked questions. But something changed. The people at my table now know I exist, and next week I'll go again — and the second time will be much easier than the first.

**The lesson is simple: small talk is not about English. It's about being there.** The English comes later, and it comes by itself. The hardest part was not the words. It was the twelve thirty.

---

## 🧠 Story vocabulary & grammar
- **Past-tense verbs you just read:** `was · came · said · finished · sent · walked · ordered · told · came · learned · laughed · asked · stopped · expected · changed · went · did · said`
- This story needed almost **no modal verbs** — that is the point. Real small talk is simple past and simple present. You can do this level of English today.
- Notice the **contractions** everywhere: *I'm, that's, didn't, I'd, I'll, it's, I'm not*. Everyday speech is full of them, and they make you sound natural, not broken.
