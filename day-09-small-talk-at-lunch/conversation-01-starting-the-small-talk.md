# Day 9 · Conversation 1 — Starting the Small Talk

## 🎬 Situation
Lunch is the easiest place to practise English, because nobody is testing you. The only skill here is **starting** — the first two sentences. You don't need a good topic, you just need to ask a question.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Colleague:** Hey Thach, are you coming to lunch with us?

> **You:** Sure, I'd love to. What time are you going?

> **Colleague:** About twelve thirty. We're going to that new place near the office.

> **You:** Sounds good. I've never eaten there. Is it good?

> **Colleague:** Really good, and it's cheap. I go there almost every week.

> **You:** Nice. Then I'll follow you guys.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`lunch · sure · time · new · place · near · office · good · never · cheap · almost · every week · follow · guys`

## ✅ After you speak
- The starting formula = **say yes + ask about the plan + ask one question**:
  *"Sure, I'd love to. What time are you going?"*
- 💡 **"I'd love to."** — the warm way to accept an invitation. *full* (I really want to) vs. *I'd like to* (polite, more neutral). Use "I'd love to" with people you like.
- 💡 **"I've never eaten there."** = in your life, never. Simple and it invites them to tell you more. Use it for any new place, tool, or restaurant.
- 💡 **"Then I'll follow you guys."** — natural way to say "I'll go with you." *follow someone* = go after them.
- 🎙️ Play `colleague-01` and answer. **Today: say "Sure, I'd love to" to one real lunch invitation.** This is the single most useful sentence in this whole day.
