# Day 9 · Conversation 4 — Talking About Your Own Work (Without Boring People)

## 🎬 Situation
Someone asks what you do. This is the dangerous question at lunch — you can talk for ten minutes about your database and lose everyone. The skill is **one sentence of work** plus a question back. Keep it short, make it human.

## 🗣️ Dialogue — read out loud (both roles), then play the audio and answer

> **Colleague:** By the way, what do you do here exactly?

> **You:** I work on the backend side. I mostly fix and build things with the database and the API.

> **Colleague:** Okay. Is it interesting so far?

> **You:** Yeah, it is. I'm still learning, but I like that the problems are real.

> **Colleague:** That's good. What's the hardest part for you right now?

> **You:** Honestly, English. The code is fine, but explaining my decisions is still hard for me.

## 🧠 Your active vocabulary — all words you already know
`exactly · backend · mostly · build · database · API · interesting · yeah · still · learning · problems · real · hardest part · right now · honestly · code · fine · explaining · decisions · hard`

## ✅ After you speak
- The work-answer formula = **area + one plain example + a human reaction**:
  *"I work on the backend side. I mostly fix and build things with the database and the API."*
- 💡 **"The code is fine, but explaining my decisions is still hard for me."** — a beautiful sentence for you. It's honest, it's a real compliment to the job, and it turns a weakness into a specific skill you're building. **Say this in your real next interview.**
- 💡 **"What's the hardest part for you right now?"** — a great question for small talk. People love talking about their own problems.
- 💡 **"Yeah"** = casual *yes*. With small talk, "Yes." sounds stiff. "Yeah" and "Sure" sound natural with people you already know.
- 🎙️ Play `colleague-04` and answer. **Today: give your one-sentence work answer out loud, three times, until it's automatic.**
- 🧠 **Your mental model:** in small talk, **breadth beats depth**. One sentence about your work + a question is the recipe. Depth is for the technical interview, not the lunch table.

## ✅ Done when…
- [ ] I described my job in ONE sentence (no details)
- [ ] I said the "code is fine, but explaining decisions is hard" line out loud

## 🔁 Bonus — do this before any job interview
Say this out loud until it feels normal. Then use it in your real next interview:

> *"I work on the backend side — mostly databases, APIs, and performance. I'm at a level where I can build and fix things, and now I'm pushing myself to explain my decisions clearly, because that's what senior engineers do."*

That last clause is your bridge to a senior role. Practise it. 💡
